# NZZ readings December 1979 to December 1982: notes

Read by eye on 2026-10-02 from the scanned issues in `data/manual/scans/nzz/`; output `nzz-readings-1979-1982.csv` (798 rows, 22 series/class combinations, 36 of 37 months).
The scans of this period are JPEG (DCT) images, not Flate, so the shared `scan.mjs` could not decode them; a small Python/PIL cropper was used instead (outside the repo).

## Table layouts

All issues are 32 to 104 PDF pages; PDF page = printed page number.

- **Swiss shares.** Running-text list "Schweizer Aktien" under the headline "Kurse vom <Tag>", "Zuerich", "Mitgeteilt von J. Vontobel & Co., Bankiers", on the
  business pages (PDF page 17 to 25 depending on the issue, two pages before the New York list). Each line: `Name (Vortag) trade trade ... R trade ...`.
  The bracketed number is the previous day's close, "R" ends the ring session, figures after it are later trades, tick marks `t`, `tt`, `ttt`, `L` are
  as printed and ignored, `dt` marks a block trade (ignored). There is no "Schluss" column in these years. I record the last figure of the sequence as the close.
  Only the Vortag in brackets means no trade that day: nothing recorded.
- **New York.** "New Yorker Boersenkurse vom <Tag>", columns Vortag, Eroeff., Hoechst, Tiefst, Vol, P/E, Schl. (close). Schlumberger and IBM are bold-face rows. Eighths as fractions.
- **Frankfurt.** "Frankfurt/Duesseldorf/Muenchen" in the foreign-markets page, columns Vortag, Schluss, one combined list (from about mid-1982 "Duesseldorf"), listing BMW, Daimler-Benz, Siemens.
- **Tokio.** List has no Industrial Bank of Japan in the issues checked (1980-01, 1981-01, 1982-06): banks listed are Bank of Tokyo, Fuji Bank and a few more. IBJ was therefore not read for any month.
- Pages used per issue are in the csv. Swiss page is usually NY page minus 2, Frankfurt NY page plus 1.

## What was done for the dates

- Monday issues (1981-08-03, 1981-12-01, 1982-05-03) print the Friday tables; the date was taken from the header "Kurse vom ..." in every case.
- 1980-01-03: the Swiss exchanges were closed on 2 January (Berchtoldstag), the page prints only a blurb for Basel; the Frankfurt table is headed "geschlossen" and only
  has a Vortag column of unstated date. So **1979-12 is only available for New York**: Vortag of the 2 January table (31 December, taken as the Vortag of a 2 January
  table). Swiss and Frankfurt 1979-12 are **not read** (the issue 1979-12-31 is a Monday issue with no stock tables at all; the Saturday 29 December issue was not downloaded).
  Frankfurt Vortag values seen once in the 1980-01-03 issue (Siemens 257, Daimler-Benz 242.50) were not recorded: date unstated, single reading, and they differ
  from the JdG prints for 28 December (258.5 and 243).
- 1980-04, 1980-07, 1981-04: the issue date follows a holiday (1 May, 1 August); the New York tables are headed with the first day of the next month and the month end was taken from the Vortag column; Frankfurt closed ("geschlossen") on 1 May 1980, Vortag taken, quote date assumed.
  1981-04 Frankfurt: the table is headed "30. April / 1. Mai"; the Schluss column was taken as 30 April (Frankfurt closed on 1 May; not printed).
- 1982-12: the Swiss and Frankfurt table of the issue of 31 December is headed "Kurse vom 30. Dezember" (30 December = last trading day, JdG agrees). New York 31 December
  was read from the issue of 3 January 1983 (page 13, "Kurse vom 31. Dezember", Vortag = 30 December close read in the other issue). The Dec-1982 Swiss readings
  are of 30 December.

## Missing months and cells

- **1982-05, all series**: the issue downloaded for it (1982-06-01) contains the tables of 31 May only for Tokyo/London/etc. (p. 13). 31 May 1982 was Whit Monday: Swiss, Frankfurt and New York were closed and the month-end tables (28 May) were printed in the Saturday 29 May issue, which was not downloaded.
- **1979-12**: Swiss and Frankfurt, see above. IBM and Schlumberger present.
- Nestle I and N 1982-12: the lines end `R 3850 TZ 382` and `2300 TZ 229`, where "TZ" is not explained and the digits after it are cut off by the line wrap; the figures before TZ (3850 / 2300) might be the close or not. Left out.
- Nestle I 1981-07 (issue 1981-08-03), Ciba-Geigy N 1981-10, Sandoz N 1981-09 and Ciba-Geigy PS 1980-01: not recorded in the first pass (line not found in the crops) and not re-read for lack of capacity; the original lines may well be readable.
- Ciba-Geigy I 1981-08: the Ciba line is cut off at the top of the column in the issue of 1981-09-01 (page 18); the bearer line is not readable.
- Sandoz I 1982-08 and 1982-09: not printed in the Swiss list of those days (no bearer line, only Na. and PS).

## Sanity checks and controls

- **IBM vs `data/raw/yahoo/ibm.csv`** (adjusted, two 2:1 splits after the period): printed / Yahoo = 4.184 on average over 36 months, range 4.139 to 4.238; the spread is the dividend adjustment plus last-trading-day differences (e.g. 1980-12 4.238, 1981-06 4.139). No month outside 1.5 percent of the mean.
- **Schlumberger vs `slb-raw.csv` (JdG print of the same New York close)**: 1979-12 and 1980-01 to 1981-11 identical in all 24 months compared; 1981-12 NZZ 55.625, slb-raw 55.75 (the JdG 4 Jan 1982 issue cited in slb-raw itself says 55.625 for 30 Dec: NZZ agrees with that). 1982-12 has no JdG value.
- **Frankfurt vs `*-raw.csv` rows "JdG print"** (10 months each for BMW, Daimler-Benz, Siemens): 10 of 28 exact; 18 differ. The differences are small: median 0.5 DM (0.2 percent), largest BMW 1980-03 +2.0 (159.50 vs 157.5), Siemens 1980-03 +1.5, Mercedes 1980-03 -1.0, Mercedes 1982-09 -1.0. All differences are listed in the note column. The NZZ list is a combined Frankfurt/Duesseldorf/Muenchen list and the JdG print a Frankfurt column, so the last prices need not be the same; I could not find a wrong date or wrong row (the headers agree and the Vortag columns fit). The rows are kept but flagged; use them with this caveat or prefer the JdG print where both exist. This is the one control that did not pass cleanly.
- No Nestle control exists in this period (Yahoo from 1990, JdG 1985).
- **Jumps by a factor of two or more**: Siemens 1980-01 -> 1980-02 (2660 -> 270: decimal point missing in the print of 31 Jan 1980, Vortag 2635); Schlumberger 3-for-2 splits Oct 1980 (143 -> 108.125) and Jul 1981 (94.625 -> 67.5), both documented in slb-raw (JdG issues of 8 to 22 October 1980 and 8 to 15 July 1981). All other month-to-month moves of the Swiss shares stay below 20 percent; those above 15 percent were checked against the Vortag columns: Swissair N 1980-10 -> 11 (690 -> 585, Vortag 620 on the day), Ciba-Geigy I 1980-02 -> 03 (1160 -> 975), Sandoz I 1981-01 -> 02 (3400 -> 3950, Vortag 3925), Sandoz PS 1980-02 -> 03 (512 -> 425), Ciba-Geigy PS 1982-11 -> 12 (1080 -> 1290, long sequence in the table, Vortag 1305), Mercedes 1981-05 -> 06 (305.5 -> 353.5, Vortag 346) and 1981-11 -> 12 (342.5 -> 285, Vortag 288).
- Class breaks: Swiss "Namen" and "Inhaber" are separate rows throughout, no unification in this period. SKA bearer line includes the PS from April 1982 (printed "inkl. PS"); the earlier SKA I figures are bearer only. Not seen: the line before April 1982 had a separate SKA PS: it is not recorded (not on the task list).

## Double reading

Every Swiss, New York and Frankfurt row was read in a first pass and again in a second crop of the same table made at a different region offset, without looking at the first values (1980-01 to 1980-07 in two crops of the same size, 1980-08 onward with pass A and pass B). Where the two crops differed I looked again at higher magnification; I did not keep a count of the small first-pass misreads, all resolved by the re-read (a printing oddity: the SBV Vortag of 1980-11 is printed "6388", plainly 388). No reading stayed unresolved except the cases under "Missing". The Vortag in brackets next to each sequence served as an independent plausibility check on the close, and for New York the Vortag/Schluss pair across two issues (Dec 1982).

## Error rate (honest estimate)

Transcription errors in the numbers: below 1 percent (digit-level readings at full resolution, two passes). The larger uncertainty is the convention "last figure of the running sequence = close" for the Swiss shares, which I could not test against any official close in this period (no Swiss control exists for 1980 to 1982; Schlumberger and IBM in New York matched, Frankfurt matched to within 0.2 percent on average). A mistaken choice of the last figure would affect long sequences with several block trades or "R"/after-hours trades (Ciba-Geigy, Nestle, Sandoz); expect an error of one to two percent in a few such rows. Frankfurt rows are the least certain (see above).
