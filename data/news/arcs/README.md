# Story arcs

The arcs are the narrative bible of the game: one document per company and one per market
theme, written **with hindsight** for the people who curate the news. The monthly news items
that players read are derived from the arcs' beats and are written **without** hindsight.
`movers.md` (generated by `npm run movers -w data`) lists the price moves every arc must explain.

## Files

For each arc `<id>` (a company id from `data/src/universe.ts`, or a theme id such as `oil`):

- `<id>.md`: the narrative, for maintainers. Hindsight allowed.
- `<id>.beats.json`: the dated beats, machine-readable, the source of the news items.

### `<id>.md`

```
# <Name>: <one-line summary of the trajectory>

Span: <first month in the game> to <last month or "today">.

## The story
Three to six paragraphs: where the company (or theme) came from, what drove it, how it ended
or where it stands. Written for an adult curator, with hindsight, plain English.

## Best and worst moments
A short list: the peak, the worst stretch, the turning points, each with month and the price
move (from movers.md).

## Unexplained moves
Months from movers.md for which no cause could be found, with a line on what was checked.

## Sources
The main sources used, with URLs or identifiers.
```

### `<id>.beats.json`

A JSON array in ascending month order:

```json
[
  {
    "month": "1985-08",
    "title": "Berkshire closes its textile mills",
    "what": "What was public by the end of that month, in two to four sentences, no later knowledge. This is the raw material of the news item.",
    "why": "For the curator: why this beat matters in the arc, hindsight allowed. One or two sentences.",
    "assets": ["brk"],
    "move": { "asset": "brk", "pct": -4 },
    "importance": 2,
    "source": "https://... (read 2026-10-03)"
  }
]
```

- `month`: when the event became public. An event that spans months gets the beat in the month of its milestone.
- `what`: facts as of the end of the month, no lookahead: no later names, outcomes or figures. Numbers where they tell the story.
- `why`: hindsight is fine here; it never reaches the players.
- `assets`: company ids concerned (empty for a pure world beat). Only companies in the game by that month (see `listed` in universe.ts and the first month in `data/out/<id>.json`).
- `move`: optional, the price move this beat explains, as in movers.md: the asset id (`sp500` and `gold` allowed) and the month's change in percent, rounded. Give it only when the connection is real; a beat may have none.
- `importance`: 1 = must be in the game (defines the arc), 2 = should be, 3 = nice to have. Aim for roughly a third each.
- `source`: where the facts come from, with the date read. Nothing from memory alone.

## Coverage rules

- Every company arc covers the company's whole time in the game, from its listing month to its exit or today: a beat at least every two years, more where things happened, and the listing itself (why someone might buy it then) and the exit (takeover, merger, bankruptcy) as beats of importance 1.
- Every month in the company's "Biggest months" list in movers.md either has a beat with a `move`, or is listed under "Unexplained moves" in the `.md` with what was checked.
- Market arcs: a beat for every market month of ±8% or more within the theme's reach, and for the calendar-year turns that the theme explains.
- Twenty to forty beats per company arc over a long life, fewer for a short one; thirty to sixty for a market arc.
- Names as at the time (`name` and `renames` in universe.ts); `data/src/profiles.ts` shows what was known when.
- Existing news items for the 1980s in `data/news/19xx.json` may be mined for facts and sources, but every beat is checked against its source.

## Ids and pictures

Every beat carries an `id`, eight hex characters, assigned once and never changed; it is how
pictures, curator decisions and later tooling refer to a beat even when its month or title
is edited. Pictures live in sidecar files, `<id>.images.json` next to the beats:

```json
[
  {
    "beat": "3f9a1c2e",
    "file": "news/aapl/1984-01-macintosh.jpg",
    "caption": "Steve Jobs presents the Macintosh, January 1984",
    "source": "https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:...",
    "licence": "CC BY-SA 3.0, photo: Name",
    "note": "optional: why this picture, or what could not be found"
  }
]
```

`file` is a path under `web/static/`, so the client loads it as `/news/aapl/1984-01-macintosh.jpg`.
Files: JPEG, PNG or WebP, at most 1600 pixels on the long side, at most 400 KB, named
`<month>-<slug>.<ext>` inside a folder per arc. One picture per beat; a beat without a picture
simply has no entry. `licence` records the terms (a Creative Commons licence with the author,
public domain, or `fair use: <rights holder>` for a press or company picture used under the
game's educational, non-commercial purpose). The build folds the sidecar into the news items.
