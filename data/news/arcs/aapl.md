# Apple Computer / Apple: the first PC star, a near-death in the 1990s, and the iPod and iPhone comeback to the most valuable company in the world

Span: 1980-12 to today.

## The story

Apple came to the market in December 1980 as the leader of the young personal computer industry, with the Apple II and the biggest stock offering since Ford in 1956. Eight months later IBM entered the market, and by 1983 the IBM PC outsold the Apple II. Apple's shares behaved like the riskiest growth stock of the era: they lost more than half from the offering to mid-1982, more than quadrupled from the summer of 1982 to May 1983 as the bull market began and the Lisa was unveiled, and lost almost two thirds again by late 1983. The Lisa (1983) flopped; the Macintosh (1984) defined Apple's identity but sold slowly at first, and in 1985 Steve Jobs lost a power struggle with John Sculley and left.

Sculley's Apple was profitable for a while. The Mac Plus, the colour Mac II and desktop publishing made 1986-1991 good years; Apple paid its first dividend in 1987 and the shares rose more than eightfold from the 1985 low to the 1991 peak. But Windows 3.0 (1990) gave cheap PCs the Mac's main advantage, Apple lost its copyright case against Microsoft (1992), and a confusing product range, inventory mistakes and the failure to build a modern operating system wore the company down under Sculley, Spindler (1993-96) and Amelio (1996-97). By early 1996 Fortune expected Apple to disappear; it held merger talks with IBM, Sun and Philips. From March 1991 to December 1997 the shares fell 81%.

The rescue began with the purchase of NeXT in December 1996, which brought Jobs back. He pushed out Amelio in July 1997, made peace with Microsoft (August 1997, +24%), cut the product line and ended the Mac clones. Apple announced a profit in January 1998 (+40% in the month), and the iMac (1998) bought time. The first comeback rally ended in September 2000, when a sales warning blamed on the G4 Cube cut the shares by 58% in a month.

The iPod (October 2001) and the iTunes Music Store (April 2003) turned Apple into a consumer-electronics company; by October 2004, with two million iPods sold in a quarter, investors understood, and the shares rose tenfold in three years. The iPhone (announced January 2007) made Apple the most valuable company in the world: it passed Microsoft in May 2010, set a record value in August 2012 and became the first trillion-dollar US company in August 2018. Jobs resigned in August 2011 and died in October 2011; Tim Cook turned Apple into a cash machine paying dividends and buying back shares, and built a services business on the base of iPhone users.

Under Cook the shares rose roughly twentyfold, with setbacks (the 40% fall of 2012-13, the China warning of January 2019) and milestones (two trillion dollars in August 2020). Apple was seen as late to artificial intelligence; Apple Intelligence (2024) and a new AI Siri (2026) were its answers. In April 2026 Apple named John Ternus to succeed Cook, and he took over on 1 September 2026, with the shares at a record.

## Best and worst moments

- 1982-05 to 1983-05: best twelve months, +312%; 1982-08 +33% and 1982-10 +39% as the bull market began; 1983-01 +37% with the Lisa.
- 1983-09: -38%, the end of the 1983 boom (cause not found).
- 1987-01: +37%; 1987-10: -32% in the crash.
- 1991-03 to 1997-12: worst stretch, -81%.
- 1997-08: +24% on the Microsoft deal; 1998-01: +40% when Apple announced it was profitable again.
- 2000-09: -58% after the sales warning blamed on the G4 Cube.
- 2001-01: +45% rebound after the surprise rate cut.
- 2004-10: +35% when two million iPods a quarter showed the iPod effect.
- 2008-01: -32% on a cautious forecast in a falling market; 2008-09: -33% in the financial crisis.
- 2018-08: first trillion-dollar US company; 2020-08: two trillion dollars.
- 2026-09: John Ternus takes over; shares at a record.

## Unexplained moves

- 1983-09: -38% (market +1%). Checked the History of Apple, Apple Lisa and Apple III articles on Wikipedia and the existing 1983 news items: the Lisa's sluggish sales and losses, the Apple III's failure and the IBM PC overtaking the Apple II in 1983 are documented, but no dated profit warning or event in September 1983 was found.
- 1987-01: +37% (market +13%). The market rose 13% in January 1987 and Apple's Mac Plus business was strong, but no Apple-specific event for the month (such as quarterly results) was found in the sources read.
- 2000-05: -32% (market -2%). The Nasdaq slide of spring 2000 is the likely background; Apple's 10-K for fiscal 2000 shows profitable quarters in the first half of the year and gives no event for May. Not explained.
