# America Online / AOL Time Warner / Time Warner: from busy signals to the deal of the century, and the long unwinding

Span: 1997-02 to 2009-11 (Time Warner spun off AOL on 9 December 2009; exit beat 2009-12). Price data 1997-02 to 2009-12 from the NZZ tables (manual/aol.csv); the game ends the position in November 2009, the last month end before the spin-off, and pays holders out at that price.

## The story

America Online, founded in the 1980s and led by Steve Case, became in the 1990s the way most American households went online: a dial-up service with e-mail, chat rooms and content, sold through millions of free disks and CDs. In December 1996 it switched to a flat $19.95 a month for unlimited use; usage tripled, the network jammed, and in January 1997 36 states made it offer refunds. The share enters the game in February 1997, when it first appears in the NZZ's New York table (AOL had moved from the Nasdaq to the NYSE in September 1996), near a low.

From there AOL became the great winner of the internet boom. It raised its price in 1998 and kept its members, bought CompuServe's subscribers (completed at the end of January 1998), ICQ (June 1998) and Netscape (agreed November 1998, completed March 1999), joined the S&P 500 in December 1998 and split its shares four times between March 1998 and November 1999. The share rose 755% in the twelve months to March 1999. In January 2000 it used its inflated shares to agree to buy Time Warner (tech 1f8b5752); the merger closed on 11 January 2001, when AOL's shareholders received one AOL Time Warner share each and Time Warner's 1.5.

The merger failed almost at once. Online advertising collapsed with the dot-com bust, AOL's dial-up subscribers began leaving for broadband, and the Time Warner managers took over: Levin retired in May 2002 (Parsons), Pittman left in July 2002 as the SEC began looking at AOL's advertising deals, Case gave up the chair in May 2003. AOL Time Warner wrote down $54 billion in the first quarter of 2002 and lost about $99 billion in 2002, and dropped AOL from its name in October 2003. The settlements with the Justice Department and the SEC (December 2004 and March 2005) cost a $60 million penalty, a $150 million fund for shareholder suits and a $300 million SEC penalty. Google bought 5% of AOL for $1 billion in December 2005, Carl Icahn's campaign ended in a $20 billion buyback in February 2006, and AOL gave its service away in August 2006 to live on advertising. Under Jeff Bewkes (CEO from January 2008) Time Warner spun off Time Warner Cable (March 2009, with a 1-for-3 reverse split) and finally AOL (December 2009), which is where the game pays holders out.

## Best and worst moments

- Listing: 1997-02 at 2.34 (on the basis of the November 1999 split).
- Best months: 1998-12 (+83%), S&P 500; 1999-03 (+64%), the internet frenzy and the Netscape closing (told in the Netscape exit beat 3880cc5a, which carries the move); 2001-01 (+51%), merger approval; 1998-11 (+38%), Netscape and Sun; 1998-09 (+36%), rebound.
- Worst months: 1998-08 (-30%), the August 1998 sell-off; 2000-01 (-26%), the Time Warner deal (tech 1f8b5752); 1999-06 (-23%).
- Best twelve months: +755% from 1998-03 to 1999-03. Worst stretch: -82% from 1999-12 to 2003-02 on the file basis (the Time Warner Cable spin-off of March 2009 is folded into the earlier prices by the build).

## Unexplained moves

- 1999-06 (-23%, market +5%): no AOL-specific cause found. Checked CNNfn of 1 June 1999 (AOL buys Nullsoft and Spinner; AOL down with other internet shares), 3 June (Microsoft targets the AOL-Netscape deal in the antitrust trial), 24 June (an internet fund manager leaves; AOL, Amazon and Yahoo "hit hard in the recent sell-off") and 30 June (MSN and AOL PC rebates). It reads as a sector sell-off of internet shares.
- 2002-10 (+26%, market +9%) and 2003-04 (+26%, market +8%): the market rebounds after the lows of October 2002 and March 2003; no beat, the moves are within 20 points of the market.
- 2000-01 (-26%): not unexplained; it is carried by the existing beat tech 1f8b5752 (AOL agrees to buy Time Warner), which has `aol` in its assets and the move.
