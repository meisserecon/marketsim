# Boeing: the great cyclical, from the jumbo era to the 737 MAX disaster

Span: 1979-12 (in the game from the start) to today.

## The story

Boeing enters the game as the world's airliner maker, with the 727, 737 and 747 in service and two new fuel-saving jets, the 767 and 757, in development. It is the purest cyclical in the game: it lives on airline orders, which follow fuel prices, interest rates and the economy, and on US defence budgets. In 1981 the stock halves as recession and record interest rates hit the airlines; in 1982 it rebounds 51%, with the biggest month in its history in August (+48%) when Wall Street's great rally begins, just as the first 767 is delivered. The mid-1980s are a boom of orders (737-300, 747-400), interrupted by the 1987 crash (-24%) and a strike in 1989 caused by too much work rather than too little.

The 1990s bring the 777 (launched by United in 1990, in service 1995), the 737 Next Generation (1993) and, in December 1996, the merger with McDonnell Douglas that makes Boeing America's only airliner maker and a defence giant. The boom then turns into a production meltdown: in October 1997 Boeing has to stop its 747 line to catch up, and the Asian crisis forces production cuts in December 1998 (-20%). The September 2001 attacks, carried out with four Boeing jets, crush airline demand (-35%) and 20,000 to 30,000 jobs go. A tanker procurement scandal costs chief executive Phil Condit his job in December 2003, and Airbus overtakes Boeing as the leading airliner maker.

The answer is the 787 Dreamliner, launched in April 2004 with a record order from ANA. It sells faster than any Boeing before, and the stock peaks in July 2007 when the first one is unveiled, but the programme is years late: first flight in December 2009, first delivery in September 2011, a fleet grounding over battery fires in January 2013. In between, a strike and the financial crisis halve the stock in 2008, with the low in February 2009 (-26%).

From 2013 Boeing turns into a cash machine: record deliveries of 737s and 787s, huge buybacks, the stock up 81% in 2013 and 89% in 2017, peaking at 440 dollars in February 2019. The MAX, a re-engined 737 rushed out to answer the Airbus A320neo, ends that. Two crashes, Lion Air in October 2018 and Ethiopian in March 2019, kill 346 people; the plane is grounded worldwide for 20 months, the CEO is fired, and the pandemic arrives on top: in March 2020 Boeing suspends its dividend and the stock falls 46%, its worst month. The vaccine news and the MAX's return to service in November 2020 bring the second-best month (+46%), but cash drains, the 777X slips, and by September 2022 the stock is 72% below its peak.

The recovery of 2023 (+37%, helped by a 90-plane Emirates 777X order in November) ends on 5 January 2024 when a door plug blows out of an Alaska Airlines MAX 9 (-19%). Calhoun leaves, Boeing buys back its fuselage supplier Spirit AeroSystems, Kelly Ortberg takes over in August 2024, a seven-week machinists' strike stops production, and Boeing raises 21 billion dollars of new capital in October 2024. Since then the story is slow repair, helped by political orders (a fighter contract in March 2025, a 200-plane Chinese order in May 2026), with the share price still far below its 2019 peak.

## Best and worst moments

- 1980-01: +29% (unexplained, see below).
- 1981: -49%, the worst calendar year.
- 1982-08: +48%, the best month; 1982-10: +32%; best twelve months +195% from 1982-06 to 1983-06.
- 1987-10: -24%, Black Monday.
- 1998-12: -20%, Asian crisis production cuts.
- 2001-09: -35%, the September 11 attacks.
- 2007-07: the 2000s peak, 787 unveiled.
- 2009-02: -26%, the financial crisis low.
- 2019-02: all-time high; 2019-03: MAX grounded (-13%).
- 2020-03: -46%, the worst month; 2020-06: +26%; 2020-11: +46%, vaccine and MAX ungrounded.
- 2022-09: -24%, the bottom of the worst stretch (-72% from 2019-02); 2022-11: +26%.
- 2023-11: +24%, Emirates order.
- 2024-01: -19%, door plug blowout; 2024-09 strike (-12%); 2024-10 capital raise.

## Unexplained moves

- 1980-01 (+29%, market +6%): no Boeing-specific event found. Checked the Wikipedia articles on Boeing, History of Boeing, Boeing 757 and Boeing 767, and the existing news items for 1979-12 and 1980-01 in data/news. The context is the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (late December 1979), a strong rise in gold (+48%) and talk of higher defence spending, plus Boeing's large order book for the 757 and 767; none of this is documented in a source read as the cause. The UPI archive search was blocked and the session's web search budget was used up.
- 2022-11 (+26%, market +5%): Boeing held an investor conference on 2 November 2022 (announced in a Boeing release of 13 October 2022), but no release or filing with its content was found on Boeing's newsroom or SEC EDGAR, so the targets presented there could not be confirmed. The market rose 5% and the Fed's pace of rate rises was in focus. Likely cause: the investor day's cash flow targets plus the market; unconfirmed.
