# BMW: the sporty upstart of the 1980s, humbled by Rover, rebuilt as a premium-only group, and caught by China's slump

Span: 1981-03 to today.

## The story

When players can first buy it in March 1981, BMW is a fast-growing, family-controlled maker of sporty saloons and motorcycles: the Quandts saved it from bankruptcy in 1959 and Eberhard von Kuenheim has run it since 1970. The new 3 Series (1982), the 7 Series (1986) and motor-sport glory (a BMW engine wins the 1983 Formula One title) make it a favourite of the 1980s boom. Foreign investors drive German shares up in 1985 and 1986, and BMW more than doubles between April 1985 and April 1986 (+115%), with +25% in August 1985 and +40% in August 1986. The crash of October 1987 takes almost a third off (-29%). Reunification (December 1989) and the Gulf crisis (September 1990) bring the next swings.

In 1993 Bernd Pischetsrieder takes over, and in January 1994 he buys the Rover Group from British Aerospace for 800 million pounds, believing BMW must become a volume maker to survive. Rover never makes money: by 1996 Der Spiegel reports losses at Rover and the aero-engine venture, Spartanburg teething problems and Kuenheim's distrust. The strong pound (above 3 marks by the end of 1999) and an ageing model range turn Rover into a sink for some 9 billion marks. In February 1999 the Rover crisis topples both Pischetsrieder and his rival Wolfgang Reitzle, and takeover speculation follows. In January 2000 the share falls 26% as Rover's future is openly questioned; in March 2000 Joachim Milberg breaks Rover up (Rover to the Phoenix consortium, Land Rover to Ford, the Mini kept), and the share jumps 23%.

The 2000s are the "premium only" recovery: the new Mini (2001), Rolls-Royce from Goodwood (2003), new models and the first Chinese plant with Brilliance in Shenyang (2004), under Helmut Panke and Norbert Reithofer. The financial crisis is the worst stretch (-64% from May 2007 to January 2009; -34% in October 2008 in dollars, made worse by a falling euro). The post-crisis boom makes BMW the best DAX share of 2010 (+85%, +32% in September alone as it joins the Euro Stoxx 50). The euro crisis (2011) and the ECB's rescue loans (January 2012, +27%) follow.

The 2010s and early 2020s bring the diesel scandal (the X3 report of September 2015), Brexit (June 2016, the Mini is built in Oxford), cartel allegations (2017), Oliver Zipse (2019), COVID (March 2020) and the vaccine rally (November 2020, +28%), then record profits from scarce cars in 2021-2022. From 2024 China turns against German luxury cars: a brake recall and Chinese weakness force a warning in September 2024, the war with Iran adds an energy shock in March 2026, and after Milan Nedeljković takes over in May 2026 a profit warning in June sends the share down 25%. In September 2026 it reaches its lowest since 2020 before a strategy day that promises a slow margin recovery. Over the whole game BMW gains about 3,200% in dollars, well ahead of Mercedes but behind the market.

## Best and worst moments

- Best twelve months: April 1985 to April 1986, +115% (1985-08 +25%, 1986-08 +40%, the biggest month).
- Crash: 1987-10 -29%.
- Rover: 1994-01 purchase; 1999-02 boardroom crisis; 2000-01 -26%; 2000-03 +23% on the break-up.
- 1998: +27% in February (record markets), -26% in August (Asian and Russian crises).
- Worst stretch: -64% from 2007-05 to 2009-01; 2008-10 -34%.
- Recovery: 2010-09 +32%, 2012-01 +27%, 2020-11 +28%.
- China slump: 2024-09 warning; 2026-06 -25% (profit warning); 2026-09 -14%.

## Unexplained moves

- 1998-02: +27% (market +7%; in marks also about +27%). Checked Die Zeit for February 1998, which reports almost daily record highs in Frankfurt and on Wall Street ('Ohne Sinn und Verstand', 19 February 1998), and the BBC reports on BMW's bid for Rolls-Royce, which was accepted only in late March/April. DaimlerChrysler's predecessor rose 16% the same month. The general boom explains part of the move, but no BMW-specific cause for the size of the jump was found.
