# Berkshire Hathaway: Warren Buffett's compounding machine, from 260 dollars a share to 750,000

Span: 1980-03 (first month of Yahoo's history) to today.

## The story

Berkshire Hathaway enters the game in March 1980 as an odd little holding company in Omaha: a failing New England textile mill that Warren Buffett had turned into a vehicle for insurance companies (National Indemnity, a growing stake in GEICO), See's Candies, the Buffalo Evening News and a portfolio of shares. It pays no dividend and never splits its stock, so one share costs a few hundred dollars and, by the end, three quarters of a million. The best guide to the arc is Buffett's yearly letter, dated late February or early March, which reports the change in book value per share: +40% for 1982, +48% for 1985, +44% for 1989.

The 1980s were the years of big minority stakes and 'white squire' deals: Capital Cities' purchase of ABC (March 1985, +22% that month), Salomon (1987), Coca-Cola (revealed in the 1988 letter) and Gillette (1989), alongside wholly owned businesses such as Nebraska Furniture Mart (1983) and Scott Fetzer (1986). The textile mills closed in 1985. The best twelve months were September 1982 to September 1983 (+125%), and the 1987 crash (-21% in October) was only a pause. Berkshire's thinly traded share price often jumped by 20% or more in a month without any news, which is why several of its biggest months remain unexplained.

The 1990s brought the Salomon scandal, in which Buffett ran the firm as interim chairman from August 1991; the purchase of all of GEICO (agreed August 1995) and the conversion of the Capital Cities stake into Disney shares; the Class B share (May 1996); and the all-stock purchase of General Re (June 1998), which doubled the insurance business but which Buffett later regretted. The share price peaked in mid-1998. In the technology boom Buffett refused to buy tech stocks, 1999 was his worst year, and the price fell 44% from June 1998 to February 2000; when the Nasdaq peaked on 10 March 2000 Berkshire hit its low, and March 2000 was its biggest month in the game (+30%).

In the 2000s Berkshire became a conglomerate of whole businesses (Shaw, Johns Manville, Fruit of the Loom, McLane, Clayton, MidAmerican/PacifiCorp) while absorbing the 9/11 insurance losses and a government investigation at General Re. In the 2008 crisis it was a lender of last resort (Goldman Sachs), yet its shares fell 44% from December 2007 to February 2009, the worst stretch of the arc, and Buffett called 2008 his worst year. The 'all-in bet on America', the purchase of the BNSF railway (November 2009), marked the turn to big capital-heavy businesses, followed by Lubrizol (2011), Heinz with 3G (2013), Precision Castparts (2015), Apple shares from 2016, Japan's trading houses (2020) and Alleghany (2022).

Charlie Munger died in November 2023. In August 2024 Berkshire became the first US company outside technology worth a trillion dollars. Buffett announced in May 2025 that Greg Abel would succeed him as chief executive at the start of 2026; the share price had peaked in April 2025 at about 800,000 dollars and drifted lower afterwards. Abel took over on 1 January 2026 with a cash pile above 370 billion dollars.

## Best and worst moments

- 1982-09 to 1983-09: best twelve months, +125%.
- 1985-03: +22% on the Capital Cities/ABC deal.
- 1987-10: -21% in the crash.
- 1998-06 to 2000-02: about -44%, the technology boom years; 2000-03 +30%, the biggest month, as the bubble burst.
- 2007-12 to 2009-02: worst stretch, -44%.
- 2009-11: BNSF purchase (turning point to the capital-heavy Berkshire).
- 2024-08: valued above one trillion dollars; peak price 2025-04.
- 2025-05: Buffett announces his retirement as chief executive.

## Unexplained moves

The A share was thinly traded (Buffett notes in the 1999 letter that volume was often too light for meaningful buybacks), and big monthly jumps without news were common in the early decades. For each month below, Buffett's letters, the Berkshire Hathaway Wikipedia article and SEC filings were checked; no event in that month was found.

- 1980-05 (+16%, market +5%): no news found; two months into the price history.
- 1983-09 (+22%, market +1%): the Blue Chip Stamps merger was completed mid-year and the Nebraska Furniture Mart purchase came later in 1983, but neither is dated to September in the sources read.
- 1985-10 (+24%, market +4%): the Scott Fetzer purchase was agreed in late 1985 and closed right after year end; the agreement date was not confirmed, so the move is not attached to it.
- 1986-02 (+22%, market +7%): the Capital Cities/ABC merger and the Scott Fetzer purchase closed around the turn of the year; no February event found.
- 1989-04 (+27%, market +5%): now a beat (annual meeting of 24 April, the first after the NYSE listing and the Coca-Cola disclosure); the causal link is inferred, no press report found.
- 1993-05 (+19%, market +2%): no event found.
- 1994-07 (+22%, market +3%): no event found.
- 1995-01 (+21%, market +2%): no event found.
- 1998-03 (+20%, market +5%): the 1997 letter (dated 27 February 1998, book value +34.1%, revealing the silver purchase) came out around then, but no source ties the rise to it.
