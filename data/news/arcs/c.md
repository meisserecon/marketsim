# Citigroup: the financial supermarket that grew too big, needed the largest rescue of 2008 and spent fifteen years recovering

Span: 1998-10 to today.

## The story

Citigroup was born on 8 October 1998 from the merger of Citicorp (Citibank) and Sanford Weill's Travelers
Group (Travelers insurance, Salomon Smith Barney). It was the world's largest financial company and, strictly,
illegal: the Glass-Steagall rules forbade combining a bank and an insurer, and Citigroup started life on a
Federal Reserve waiver. Congress repealed the relevant parts of the law in November 1999, and Weill, sole
chief executive from 2000, kept buying: Associates First Capital (subprime consumer loans), Banamex in
Mexico, European American Bank. Net income rose from $11.2 billion in 1999 to $15.3 billion in 2002 and
$17.0 billion in 2004.

The boom's scandals landed on Citigroup first. Its Salomon Smith Barney had sold and cheered WorldCom and
helped finance Enron; its analyst Jack Grubman became the face of conflicted research. Between 2002 and 2005
Citigroup paid or reserved billions: $400 million in the research settlement, $2.65 billion to WorldCom
investors, $2 billion to Enron investors. Under Charles Prince, chief executive from 2003, the shares went
sideways while the bank grew into mortgage securities. In July 2007 Prince said "as long as the music is
playing, you've got to get up and dance."

The music stopped in the autumn. Citigroup held about $55 billion of subprime exposure, most of it in
"super senior" slices of CDOs that its models treated as nearly riskless. In November 2007 it warned of up to
$11 billion of write-downs and Prince resigned; in January 2008 it lost $9.8 billion and cut its dividend.
It raised capital again and again (Abu Dhabi, private investors, the public), lost Wachovia to Wells Fargo,
took $25 billion from the Treasury in October 2008 and in November needed a government guarantee on $306
billion of assets. In February 2009 the government converted its preferred shares into up to 36% of the
company. From the end of 2006 to February 2009 the stock lost 97%. The bottom came in March 2009, when chief
executive Vikram Pandit told staff the bank was profitable again; the shares rose 69% that month.

The recovery was long and slow. Citigroup repaid the rescue (December 2009) and the government sold its last
shares at a $12 billion profit (December 2010). In May 2011 it carried out a 1-for-10 reverse split; the
game's prices are adjusted for it, so news before May 2011 quoted prices one tenth of the game's. Repeated
failures in the Fed's stress tests (2012, 2014), a fraud at Banamex and in 2020 regulatory orders over
"longstanding deficiencies" in risk controls showed how hard the giant was to manage. Under Jane Fraser,
chief executive from 2021, Citigroup left consumer banking in most foreign countries, reorganised around five
businesses (2023) and started selling Banamex. By 2025-2026 profits were the highest in years and the shares
were back around $100-140 (split-adjusted), still far below the 2006 peak of about $557. The lesson: being too
big to fail saved the bank, not its shareholders' money.

## Best and worst moments

- 1999-10: +23%, Congress nears repeal of Glass-Steagall.
- 2000-08: the early peak, about $542 (split-adjusted); 2006-12: the all-time high, about $557.
- 2002-10: +25%, relief rally after the scandal summer; Q3 profit holds up.
- 2007-11: -21%, $8-11 billion write-down warning, Prince resigns.
- 2008-06: -23%, sector sell-off after Lehman's loss.
- 2008-10: -33%, Wachovia lost, first $25 billion from the Treasury, Q3 loss.
- 2008-11: -39%, government rescue and guarantee.
- 2009-01: -47%, $8.3 billion loss, split into Citicorp and Citi Holdings.
- 2009-02: -58%, government takes up to 36%; the bottom at about $15 (split-adjusted).
- 2009-03: +69%, Pandit memo: profitable again.
- 2009-08: +58% (unexplained).
- 2011-10: +23%, Q3 profit and market rally after the August-September euro sell-off.
- 2019-01: +24%, rebound after a 20% fall in December 2018.
- 2020-03: -34%, pandemic crash.
- 2020-11: +33%, vaccine rally.
- 2026-04: +13%, Q1 profit up 42%.
- Worst stretch: -97% from 2006-12 to 2009-02. Best twelve months: +127% from 2009-02 to 2010-02.

## Unexplained moves

- 2009-08 (+58%, market +3%): no Citigroup announcement in August 2009 among its 8-K filings (the
  exchange of preferred for common shares closed in late July, second-quarter results came on 17 July).
  The move came during a broad rally in the most-hit financial stocks after the share swap tripled the
  number of tradable shares, but no source read here explains it. Checked: Citigroup 8-K exhibits
  July-October 2009 (EDGAR full-text search), Wikipedia "Citigroup".
