# Schweizerische Kreditanstalt / CS Holding / Credit Suisse: the boldest of the Swiss banks, ruined by its investment bank and its scandals

Span: 1982-03 to 2023-06.

## The story

The Schweizerische Kreditanstalt (SKA, Credit Suisse abroad) entered the game still marked by the Chiasso scandal of 1977, which had cost it about 1.4 billion francs, and tied since 1978 to the US investment bank First Boston. Under Rainer Gut, chairman from 1983, it was the most ambitious of the three big Swiss banks. It rode the 1985 Zurich boom (best twelve months +126% from February 1985), took control of First Boston after the 1987 crash and junk-bond troubles (CS First Boston, 1988; another $300 million of equity in 1990), put itself under a listed holding, CS Holding, in 1989, and won Bank Leu in 1990 in the first hostile takeover in Swiss banking. When the property crash broke the Volksbank, Gut and Josef Ackermann took it over in January 1993 for 2.6 billion francs, beating the Bankgesellschaft at the last minute.

In April 1996 Gut proposed a merger to the Bankgesellschaft and was rebuffed; twenty months later the Bankgesellschaft merged with the Bankverein instead, and Credit Suisse found itself the smaller of two Swiss big banks. Lukas Mühlemann's answer was "bancassurance": the renamed Credit Suisse Group (1997) bought the insurer Winterthur in August 1997 and, at the top of the market, the US investment bank Donaldson, Lufkin & Jenrette in November 2000. The 1998 crisis showed the risk (CSFB lost about $1.3 billion in Russia; the share fell 30% in August and 37% in September, then rose 39% in October), and 2001-2002 made it real: Winterthur's capital melted with the stock market, the bank was tied to the Swissair collapse, and 2002 brought a net loss of 3.3 billion francs. Oswald Grübel, first with John Mack and from July 2004 alone, cleaned up the group; Winterthur was sold to AXA in 2006.

Credit Suisse came through 2008 without state aid: it raised 10 billion francs from existing shareholders, Qatar among them, on the day the state rescued UBS, and earned 6.7 billion francs in 2009 (+26% in each of March and April 2009). That was its high point in reputation. Under Brady Dougan it kept a large investment bank; in May 2014 it pleaded guilty in the US to helping Americans evade taxes and paid about $2.6 billion. Tidjane Thiam (2015-2020) raised 6 billion francs, wrote off the old investment bank (a 5.8-billion-franc quarterly loss in February 2016, -25%) and was brought down by a spying affair in February 2020.

The end was a cascade. In March 2021 the Greensill supply-chain funds sold to wealthy clients ($10 billion) collapsed and the Archegos default cost 4.8 billion francs (-27% in the month). A chairman brought in to restore order, António Horta-Osório, resigned over Covid quarantine breaches in January 2022. In October 2022 rumours on social media triggered withdrawals of 110 billion francs in a quarter; a last strategy with a 4-billion-franc capital increase and the Saudi National Bank as anchor investor did not stop them. In March 2023, after material weaknesses in its accounts, the Saudi investor's "absolutely not" and a 50-billion-franc emergency loan, the Swiss government forced it into a takeover by UBS for 3 billion francs on 19 March (-71% in the month); 16 billion francs of AT1 bonds were written off. The takeover was completed on 12 June 2023. Over 41 years in the game the share lost 89% in dollars, and 99% from its April 2007 peak.

## Best and worst moments

- 1985-10 (+17%): the 1985 boom; best twelve months +126% from 1985-02 to 1986-02.
- 1993-01: the Volksbank coup.
- 1998-08 (-30%), 1998-09 (-37%), 1998-10 (+39%): Russia, LTCM, rebound.
- 2000-03 (+25%): one-year high as investors left technology shares.
- 2001-09 (-18%), 2002-07 (-23%): Winterthur and the bear market.
- 2007-04: all-time peak in the game; worst stretch -99% to 2023-05.
- 2008-10 (-23%): capital from Qatar instead of the state.
- 2009-03 (+26%), 2009-04 (+26%): crisis winner.
- 2016-02 (-25%): Thiam's write-off.
- 2020-03 (-28%), 2020-11 (+33%): Covid crash and vaccine rally.
- 2021-03 (-27%): Greensill and Archegos.
- 2023-03 (-71%): forced takeover by UBS.

## Unexplained moves

- 2003-04 (+38%): no cause found. Checked: Credit Suisse's annual reports on Form 20-F for 2002 and 2004 (EDGAR, CIK 1159510) give the 2002 loss, the co-CEO structure from January 2003 and the 2004 management changes but no April 2003 event; the encyclopedia history and Wikipedia have nothing for the month; the NZZ scans end in November 2001. The S&P 500 rose 8% in April 2003, and Credit Suisse had fallen about 21% in February-March, so a rebound from a depressed level is the likely explanation, unconfirmed.
