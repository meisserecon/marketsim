# Disney: a raid, a revival, a media empire and the streaming gamble

Span: 1979-12 (game start; listed long before) to today.

## The story

When the game opens, Walt Disney Productions is a beloved brand in decline: its parks earn the money, its film studio struggles (The Black Hole, its costliest film, opens in December 1979), and management is spending more than a billion dollars on EPCOT Center (opened October 1982). The share price barely moves from 1979 to 1984. That cheapness invited the raid of 1984: Saul Steinberg began buying in March (+34% that month), Disney paid him greenmail in June (-24%), and in September Roy E. Disney and the Bass family installed Michael Eisner and Frank Wells.

The Eisner-Wells years are the arc's first great run. Touchstone films for adults, television hits such as The Golden Girls and, from 1989, the animated hits of the 'Disney Renaissance' (The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, The Lion King) lifted the market value from about 2 billion to 22 billion dollars by 1994; the best twelve months were April 1985 to April 1986 (+134%). The October 1987 crash (-29%) was a short interruption. The weak spot was Euro Disney: chosen in 1985, opened in April 1992, it disappointed at once and was negotiating with its banks over its debts by 1994.

Wells died in April 1994, and Eisner's second decade was rockier. The 19-billion-dollar purchase of Capital Cities/ABC (July 1995) brought the ABC network and ESPN, whose cable fees became Disney's biggest profit engine, but also the Ovitz fiasco and a stagnant share price from 1997. The internet boom briefly re-rated media stocks (January 2000, +24%), then the bust, the attacks of September 2001 (-27% that month, with a forced sale by Sid Bass) and a weak ABC produced the worst stretch of the arc: -65% from April 2000 to September 2002. Roy E. Disney's revolt, Comcast's unwanted bid and a 43% protest vote in 2004 ended with Eisner handing over to Bob Iger in 2005.

Iger's first tenure (2005-2020) is the second great run: Pixar (2006), Marvel (2009) and Lucasfilm (2012) supplied the characters, ESPN the cash, and the stock rose about 371% against the S&P 500's 161%, despite the 2008-09 recession (profit down a third in late 2008). Cord-cutting showed up in 2015, and Disney answered by buying most of 21st Century Fox (agreed December 2017, closed March 2019 for 71 billion dollars) and launching Disney+ in November 2019. The investor days of April 2019 (+23%) and December 2020 (+22%) turned Disney into a streaming stock, peaking in February 2021 even though the pandemic had closed the parks in March 2020.

Then the streaming losses mounted. The stock fell about 44% in 2022, Bob Chapek was dismissed in November 2022 and Iger returned. Activist pressure from Nelson Peltz (January 2023, +25%), cost cuts and price rises brought streaming to profit by 2024 (November 2024, +22%), and strong results in May 2025 (+24%) followed. In February 2026 the board named Josh D'Amaro, head of the parks, as Iger's successor. At about 105 dollars in September 2026 the shares remain far below their 2021 peak of 189.

## Best and worst moments

- 1984-03: +34% on takeover buying; 1984-06: -24% after the greenmail payment.
- 1984-09: Eisner and Wells take over (turning point).
- 1985-04 to 1986-04: best twelve months, +134%.
- 1987-10: -29% in the crash.
- 1995-07: Capital Cities/ABC purchase agreed (turning point).
- 2000-04 to 2002-09: worst stretch, -65%; 2001-09 -27%.
- 2019-04: +23% on the Disney+ plan; 2020-12 +22% on its early success; peak 2021-02.
- 2020-03: -18% as the parks close.
- 2022: about -44%; 2022-11 Iger returns.
- 2023-01 +25%, 2024-11 +22%, 2025-05 +24% on the recovery.

## Unexplained moves

- 1975-02 (+31%): before the game starts in December 1979; not researched.
- 1985-01 (+24%, market +7%): checked the Disney history pages and the Eisner article; no specific January 1985 news found. The Eisner turnaround was under way and the market rallied, but no source ties this month to an event.
- 1987-01 (+31%, market +13%): the market's strong January 1987 explains part of it. No Disney-specific news or earnings report for the month was found in the sources read.
- 2000-01 (+24%, market -5%): AOL agreed to buy Time Warner on 10 January and Disney named Bob Iger president that month (both in a beat), but no source was found that ties Disney's rise to either; the web search budget ran out before contemporary market reports could be checked.
