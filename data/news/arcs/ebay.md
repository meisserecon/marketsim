# eBay: the profitable dot-com that peaked in 2004, shed PayPal, and came back as a cash machine

Span: 1998-09 to today.

## The story

Pierre Omidyar started AuctionWeb in September 1995 as a hobby site; renamed eBay in 1997 and run from March 1998 by Meg Whitman, it listed on the Nasdaq in September 1998 (3.5 million shares at $18, first-day close about $53, a value of nearly $1.9 billion). The pitch was unusual for an internet company: eBay held no inventory, shipped nothing, took a fee on every sale and made a profit. It had just over a million registered users and revenue of $47 million in 1998.

The first year was pure mania. The shares rose 84% in October and 138% in November 1998, 839% in the first twelve months; in April 1999 eBay sold new shares at $170 to raise about $700 million. The June 1999 outage, when the site went down and eBay credited users $3.9 million, showed the fragility behind the boom and slowed growth (-35% in July 1999). But unlike most dot-coms eBay kept growing and earning through the crash: revenue rose from $225 million in 1999 to $431 million in 2000 with a $48 million profit, and the shares rebounded sharply in early 2001 while other internet stocks kept falling.

The middle years were eBay's golden age. It bought PayPal for $1.4 billion in October 2002, expanded abroad, and by 2004 had revenue of $3.27 billion and net income of $778 million. January 2005 was the peak of eBay as a growth stock: a record quarter, but a forecast of slower growth, and the shares fell 30% in a month and 81% from the end of 2004 to February 2009. Expensive bets went wrong (Skype, bought for $2.6 billion in 2005 and mostly sold in 2009), Whitman left in 2008, and the financial crisis brought job cuts.

From 2009 PayPal carried the company. Under activist pressure from Carl Icahn eBay announced in September 2014 that it would spin off PayPal, which it did in July 2015 (the game folds the PayPal shares into the eBay price). The remaining marketplace grew slowly; eBay sold StubHub (2019) and its classifieds (2020), started paying a dividend in 2019 under investor pressure, and bought back shares on a large scale.

The pandemic brought shoppers back (+33% in April 2020), and with steady buybacks and dividends the shares reached record highs in 2025-26. In May 2026 GameStop proposed to buy eBay for about $56 billion, an offer eBay rejected. For a player, eBay is the lesson that a good business can be a poor investment at the wrong price (1999, 2004) and a good one when nobody wants it (2009).

## Best and worst moments

- Best twelve months: +839% from 1998-09 to 1999-09; best months 1998-11 (+138%), 1998-10 (+84%), 1999-04 (+52%).
- Peak of the growth years: 2004-12; then 2005-01 (-30%) and the worst stretch -81% from 2004-12 to 2009-02, with 2008-10 (-32%) in the crisis.
- Recoveries: 2001-01 (+50%) and 2001-04 (+39%) when eBay grew through the crash; 2009-04 (+31%); 2016-07 (+33%); 2020-04 (+33%).
- Turning points: 2002-10 PayPal bought; 2014-09 and 2015-07 PayPal spun off; 2019-01 first dividend.

## Unexplained moves

- 1998-11 (+138%): no eBay announcement found for November 1998 in its filings (the 10-Q for the September quarter was filed November 13 and is covered in 1998-10). The month coincides with a frenzy for internet shares (Amazon announced a three-for-one split on November 19); no source read ties eBay's jump to a specific event.
- 2000-11 (-33%): no eBay-specific event found; the 10-Q for the September quarter (filed November 9, 2000) shows continued growth. The month saw a broad collapse of technology shares (market -8%).
