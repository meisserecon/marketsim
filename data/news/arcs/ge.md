# General Electric / GE Aerospace: from Welch's empire to near-ruin, and back as a jet-engine champion

Span: 1979-12 (in the game from the start) to today. Renamed GE Aerospace in the game from 2024-04.

## The story

In 1980 General Electric is the archetypal American industrial: turbines, jet engines, locomotives, medical scanners, appliances and light bulbs, 411,000 employees and a respected chairman, Reginald Jones. In 1981 Jones hands over to Jack Welch, 45, who announces that every GE business must be number one or number two in its market or be fixed, sold or closed. In five years a quarter of the workforce goes ("Neutron Jack"), and GE buys its way into finance (Employers Reinsurance 1984, Kidder Peabody 1986) and media (RCA and NBC, December 1985, then the biggest takeover outside oil).

The formula works spectacularly. GE Capital, the lending, leasing and insurance arm, grows into the largest finance company in America and delivers smooth, rising profits that the market rewards. Apart from the 1987 crash (-23% in October), the 1990 Gulf shock and the Kidder Peabody trading fraud of 1994, the stock rises almost without pause, +37% to +52% a year from 1995 to 1999. In August 2000 GE is the most valuable company in the world, worth more than 600 billion dollars. That price (281 dollars, adjusted for later spin-offs) is not seen again until 2025.

Jeff Immelt inherits a company priced for perfection days before the September 2001 attacks. Welch's last deal, Honeywell, has been blocked by Brussels. The stock halves by 2002 and goes sideways while Immelt reshuffles (NBC Universal, Genworth, plastics sold). Then the credit crisis exposes the dependence on GE Capital, which funds itself with short-term borrowing: a profit miss in April 2008, a 15 billion dollar capital raise with Warren Buffett's help in October 2008 (-23%), doubts about the loan book in January 2009 (-25%), the dividend cut by two thirds in February 2009 (-30%, the worst month) and the loss of the triple-A rating in March. The rebound starts in April 2009 (+25%).

The decade after is a slow unwinding. GE sells NBC Universal to Comcast (2009-2013), and in April 2015 decides to sell most of GE Capital and return up to 90 billion dollars to shareholders. But the remaining industrial core is weaker than it looked: Immelt retires in 2017, his successor John Flannery halves the dividend, a hidden hole in old long-term care insurance policies appears in January 2018, the power business collapses, GE is dropped from the Dow in June 2018 after 111 years, and in October 2018 outsider Larry Culp replaces Flannery, writes off 22 billion dollars and cuts the dividend to one cent. November 2018 (-26%) and August 2019 (Harry Markopolos calls GE "a bigger fraud than Enron", -21%) mark the depths; the low of the whole arc, -89% from the 2000 peak, comes in July 2020 in the pandemic.

Culp's fix is to break the company up. Announced in November 2021, the split sends GE HealthCare off in January 2023 and GE Vernova (power and wind) in April 2024. What remains, GE Aerospace, builds and services engines found on about two thirds of the world's airliner departures (GE's 2018 annual report), and in the post-pandemic travel boom it is one of the market's best performers: +95% in 2023, +64% in 2024, +85% in 2025, with orders up 46% (January 2025, +22% month) and 74% (January 2026). The stock reaches a new all-time high in June 2026.

## Best and worst moments

- 1982-08: +14%, the great bull market begins; GE +65% in 1982.
- 1987-10: -23%, Black Monday.
- 1991-12: +18%, Fed cuts, market rally.
- 1995-1999: five straight years of +37% to +52%.
- 2000-08: the peak, most valuable company in the world.
- 2008-10: -23%, Buffett and the 15 billion dollar raise; 2009-01: -25%; 2009-02: -30%, dividend cut (worst month); 2009-04: +25%.
- 2017-11: dividend halved; 2017 -45%, 2018 -57%.
- 2018-10/11: Culp, 22 billion dollar write-off, one-cent dividend; 2018-11 -26%.
- 2019-01: +34%, the best month, relief on cash flow.
- 2019-08: -21%, Markopolos.
- 2020-03: -27%; 2020-07 the low (-89% from 2000-08); 2020-11: +37%, vaccine.
- 2022-10: +26%; 2023-01: +23%, GE HealthCare spun off; best twelve months +129% from 2022-09 to 2023-09.
- 2024-04: GE Vernova spun off, GE Aerospace remains.
- 2025-01: +22%; 2026-06: all-time high.

## Unexplained moves

- 2018-11 (-26%, market +2%): no November-specific cause confirmed. Checked GE's 8-K filings for November 2018 (only the 13 November agreements with Baker Hughes, a GE company, on selling down GE's stake), the GE 2018 annual report letter by Larry Culp, and the General Electric, GE Capital and Larry Culp Wikipedia articles. The fall continued the slide after the 30 October results (22.8 billion dollar loss, dividend cut to one cent, see the 2018-10 beat); worries about GE's debt and credit rating are the likely driver but no source read in this session documents them, and press coverage could not be searched.
