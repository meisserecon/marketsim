# Glencore: Marc Rich's trading house floats at the top, swallows Xstrata, nearly breaks in 2015, pays for its bribes and bets on coal and copper

Span: 2011-05 to today.

## The story

Glencore is the former Marc Rich + Co, founded in Switzerland in 1974 by Marc Rich and Pincus Green. Rich was indicted in the United States in 1983 on 65 counts including tax evasion and trading with Iran, fled to Switzerland, and was forced out of his own firm in 1994 after a failed attempt to corner zinc; the managers bought it and renamed it Glencore. Under Ivan Glasenberg, chief executive from 2002, it became the world's largest commodity trader (in 2010 an estimated 60% of traded zinc and half of traded copper) and also a miner, with a third of Xstrata. Allegations of dealing with sanctioned and corrupt regimes, from apartheid South Africa to Iraq's oil-for-food programme and Congo, followed it into public life.

The IPO in May 2011 raised $10bn at 530p a share and valued Glencore at $59bn, at almost exactly the top of the China commodity boom. The shares never closed a month above that price again (in dollars) through 2026. Glencore used its new paper to take over Xstrata: proposed in February 2012 as a merger of equals, the deal was forced up to 3.05 shares per Xstrata share by Qatar Holding and completed on 2 May 2013, with Glasenberg in charge and Xstrata's Mick Davis gone. Glencore wrote off $7.7bn of the price that summer.

Then commodities fell. By August 2015 copper was at a six-year low, oil had halved, and Glencore carried about $30bn of net debt. In September it suspended its dividend, sold $2.5bn of new shares at 125p, shut its African copper mines and announced a $10bn debt-reduction plan; the shares still fell 39% that month to about a sixth of the IPO price, and the company had to state that it had "absolutely no solvency issues". It survived by selling assets (40% of its agricultural business, precious-metal streams, Las Bambas earlier), cutting zinc output and getting its banks to roll over credit lines in February 2016, just as commodities bottomed. The shares rose about two and a half times in 2016, and dividends returned in 2017.

The second act was legal and climatic. US prosecutors subpoenaed Glencore in 2018 over bribery in Nigeria, Congo and Venezuela; the CFTC, the UK Serious Fraud Office, Switzerland and the Netherlands followed. In May 2022 Glencore pleaded guilty in the US and UK and paid about $1.5bn in total, plus $180m to Congo and later CHF2m and $150m in Switzerland. On climate it capped coal output in 2019 and promised in 2020 to run its coal mines down rather than sell them. Gary Nagle, the head of coal, succeeded Glasenberg in 2021, and the coal Glencore had kept made it a fortune in the 2021-22 energy crisis: record EBITDA of $34bn in 2022. Nagle then bought Teck's steelmaking coal for $9bn and, in August 2024, dropped the plan to spin coal off because shareholders wanted the cash.

Since 2025 the story is copper. Glencore promised more than a million tonnes a year by 2028; copper went to records above $13,000 a tonne; and in January 2026 Rio Tinto and Glencore held merger talks that ended in February over control and valuation. Glencore agreed to sell 40% of its Congo copper and cobalt mines to a US-backed consortium, reported strong 2026 half-year results and planned a secondary listing in Sydney. In the game it is still about 16% below its IPO price in dollars after fifteen years, a lesson in buying a commodity company at the top of the cycle.

## Best and worst moments

- IPO, 2011-05: the top; first month-end at about $8.73 in game dollars.
- Merger, 2012-02 announced; 2012-05 -24% (Grexit), 2012-08 +22%, 2012-09 sweetened to 3.05; completed 2013-05.
- Worst stretch, 2011-05 to 2016-01: -85%. Crisis months 2015-08 -30%, 2015-09 -39%; reprieves 2015-02 +24% and 2015-10 +26%.
- Turning point, 2016-02: +46%, the best month; then 2016-03 +22%, 2016-07 +22%. Best twelve months +224% from 2016-01 to 2017-01.
- Covid crash 2020-03 -38%, vaccine rally 2020-11 +40%.
- Guilty pleas 2022-05; coal windfall peak reported 2023-02.
- Coal kept, 2024-08.
- Rio Tinto talks, 2026-01: +25%.

## Unexplained moves

All twelve biggest months have a beat with a move. Two are only partly explained:

- 2012-08 (+22%): half-year results were weak and commodity prices flat. Checked Glencore's August 2012 announcements (interim results, Viterra regulatory update) and press on the Xstrata negotiations; most likely a market relief rally and positioning around a raised Xstrata ratio, but no single cause was found.
- 2016-07 (+22%): coal, nickel and zinc prices rose 8-15% in the month (World Bank data); no company announcement in July was found in the RNS listing.
