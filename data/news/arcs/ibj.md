# Industrial Bank of Japan: the bubble's most valuable bank, the Onoue scandal, the bad-loan years and Mizuho

Span: 1985-05 to 2000-08 (merged into Mizuho, paid out in cash at the last price).

## The story

The Industrial Bank of Japan was the aristocrat of Japanese banking: founded by the state in 1902
to finance industry, a long-term credit bank funding itself with debentures and lending for years
to steel, shipbuilding and car companies, with shares in many of them. It enters the game in May
1985, just before the Plaza Accord. The strong yen led the Bank of Japan to cut its discount rate
five times from January 1986 to February 1987, down to 2.5%, and cheap money inflated shares and
land. IBJ, holding both, was the purest bet on the bubble: +45% in March 1986, +69% in January
1987, four times its price within twelve months, and by 1989 ranked by some as the most valuable
company in the world, worth more than Exxon.

The Bank of Japan began raising rates in May 1989. The Nikkei peaked on 29 December 1989 and IBJ
fell 29% in March 1990 after a full-point rate rise. In August 1991 the Onoue scandal broke: an
Osaka restaurant owner reputed to predict share prices had used forged deposit certificates to
borrow from a dozen lenders, and IBJ's Osaka branch had lent her about 240 billion yen; the
chairman resigned in October. Government packages (August 1992) produced rallies but no recovery.

The bad-loan years followed: credit co-operatives failed from 1994, Hyogo Bank in 1995, and in
1997 the crisis reached the big banks. IBJ was drawn into rescuing Nippon Credit Bank in April;
Yamaichi and Hokkaido Takushoku collapsed in November. A first public capital injection in March
1998 (1.8 trillion yen) was too small. The Long-Term Credit Bank crisis took IBJ to its low in
August 1998, 92% below the 1989 peak. New laws, the nationalisation of LTCB (October 1998) and
7.5 trillion yen of public capital for 15 banks (March 1999) stabilised the system. In August 1999
IBJ agreed to merge with Fuji Bank and Dai-Ichi Kangyo into what became Mizuho, the world's
largest bank by assets; the holding company was created on 29 September 2000. In dollars the
round trip from the 1985 listing ended 49% up, but 83% down from the 1989 peak.

## Best and worst moments

- 1986-03: +45%, rate cuts; 1987-01: +69%, Tokyo past 20,000; 1987-04: +41% (unexplained).
- 1989-12: the peak, among the world's most valuable companies.
- 1990-03: -29%, the bubble bursts after a full-point rate rise.
- 1991-08: the Onoue scandal.
- 1992-08: +40%, the government's rescue package at the market low.
- 1997-01: -27%, bad-loan fears reach the big banks.
- 1989-12 to 1998-08: -92%; 1998-08: -29%, the LTCB crisis and the Russian default.
- 1998-10: +31%, nationalisation law and a 17% yen jump; 1999-03: +35%, public capital.
- 1999-08: +26%, the Mizuho merger.

## Unexplained moves

- 1986-07 (+36%): the ruling LDP won a landslide in the 6 July election and the yen rose 6.5%,
  but no source linking either to bank shares was found; most of the move was in yen.
- 1987-04 (+41%): Tokyo financial shares kept rising after the February 1987 rate cut; no
  IBJ-specific news found (Wikipedia, BIS paper, CNN and UPI archives checked).
- 1990-10 (+32%): a rebound after the September slide (-22%); the yen rose 6% in the month,
  the rest is unexplained.
- 1997-06 (+29%): no news found between the Nippon Credit Bank rescue plan (April) and its
  capital injection (July); the market rose 4%.
