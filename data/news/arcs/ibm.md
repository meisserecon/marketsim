# IBM: the unshakeable blue chip that nearly broke, was rebuilt, and became an AI bet

Span: 1979-12 (in the game from the start) to today.

## The story

In 1980 IBM is the safest stock in America. It sells or leases the mainframes on which banks, airlines and governments run, it earns most of the profit of the whole computer industry, and its only serious enemy is the US government, whose 1969 antitrust suit could break it up. In January 1982 Washington drops the case, and in August 1981 IBM has launched the Personal Computer, built in a year from outside parts with a Microsoft operating system and an Intel chip. The PC becomes the office standard and the stock rises 69% in 1982. By 1985 IBM takes an estimated 69% of the profit of the hundred largest computer companies.

The seeds of the fall are in that success. Because the PC is built from other people's parts, clone makers copy it and Intel and Microsoft collect the profits; the attempt to take control back with the PS/2 and OS/2 in 1987 fails. Cheaper PCs and networked servers eat into the mainframe. Earnings slide from above 5 billion dollars to 3 billion in 1989, and then the dam breaks: IBM's first ever loss in 1991, the warning of December 1992 that the dividend might be cut (the worst month in the game, -26%), the record loss and the dividend cut of January 1993, and John Akers' exit. From its August 1987 high to August 1993 the share loses three quarters of its value.

The board brings in an outsider, Lou Gerstner of RJR Nabisco, who keeps IBM in one piece instead of splitting it into "Baby Blues", cuts tens of thousands more jobs, recommits to the mainframe and builds services and software (Lotus 1995, PwC Consulting 2002). By late 1996 Wall Street "discovers" the recovery and the stock goes on to quadruple by mid-1999. IBM even looks like a safe haven during the dot-com crash: it jumps in January 2001 and in October 2002 (+35%, its best month) on reassuring earnings. Sam Palmisano sells the PC business to Lenovo in 2004 and runs a steady, buyback-driven company through the financial crisis.

The third act is a long disappointment. Under Ginni Rometty, IBM promises at least 20 dollars of earnings per share by 2015 (May 2010), Warren Buffett buys 5.5% of the company (revealed 2012), and the price peaks in March 2013. Then revenue shrinks, IBM pays GlobalFoundries to take its chip factories and drops the 2015 target (October 2014), and the stock is lower in 2020 than in 2011. Watson, the Jeopardy! winner, never becomes the business it was meant to be. In October 2018 a revenue miss in a falling market costs 24% in a month, and IBM answers with its largest purchase ever, Red Hat for 34 billion dollars.

Red Hat is the base of the fourth act. Arvind Krishna (CEO from April 2020) spins off the old infrastructure services as Kyndryl (2021) and sells Watson Health and the Weather Company. Software growth and an AI order book make IBM a favourite again: +34% in 2024, +35% in 2025, an all-time high in November 2025. But 2026 shows how fragile the new story is: an AI coding tool that promises to modernise COBOL sends IBM to its worst day in 25 years in February (-22% month), a quantum-and-AI rally adds 29% in May, and a warning in July that big deals failed to close takes 20% off again.

## Best and worst moments

- 1982 (calendar year, +69%): antitrust case dropped, the PC takes off.
- 1987-08: the 1980s high; Black Monday 1987-10 (-19%).
- 1992-12: -26%, job cuts and the first hint of a dividend cut; 1993-01 dividend cut and Akers out.
- 1993-08/09: the bottom, -75% from the 1987 high; Gerstner arrives 1993-04.
- 1996-11: +24%, Wall Street rediscovers IBM; best twelve months +125% from 1998-06 to 1999-06.
- 2001-01: +32% and 2001-04: +20%, the safe haven in the tech crash.
- 2002-04: -19% and 2002-09: -23%; then 2002-10: +35%, the best month.
- 2008-10: -21% in the crisis, quickly recovered (+56% in 2009).
- 2013-03: the pre-AI peak; 2014-10 (-13%) starts the long slide.
- 2018-10: -24%, then Red Hat.
- 2025-11: all-time high.
- 2026-02: -22% (Anthropic COBOL shock); 2026-05: +29% (unexplained); 2026-07: -20% (deals failed to close).
