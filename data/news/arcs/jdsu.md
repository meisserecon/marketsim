# Uniphase / JDS Uniphase / Viavi: the laser maker that became the telecom bubble's acquisition machine, lost 99%, and returned as an AI test-equipment supplier

Span: 1993-11 to today.

## The story

Uniphase started in 1979 in a San Jose garage making gas lasers for instruments in biotechnology, chip-making and printing. When it listed on the Nasdaq in November 1993 it was a small company with about $27 million of sales, a new laser system for inspecting chip wafers (Ultrapointe) and a recent patent fight behind it. A player buying in 1993 got a cheap niche technology stock. The turn came in May 1995, when Uniphase bought a maker of modulators and transmitters for fibre-optic networks; within a few years fibre-optic components, the parts that turn telephone calls and web pages into light, were almost all of its business.

In January 1999 Uniphase agreed to merge with JDS Fitel of Ottawa, and from June 30, 1999 the combined JDS Uniphase rode the telecom and internet boom like few others. It split its shares three times between July 1999 and early 2000 and used its soaring stock to buy Optical Coating Laboratory (agreed November 1999), E-TEK Dynamics (January 2000, about $15 billion) and, at the very top, SDL (July 2000, about $45 billion). Quarterly sales rose from about $60 million to more than $280 million within a year. In the twelve months to February 2000 the shares rose 1,097%.

The collapse was total. Carriers stopped spending in late 2000; on the day the SDL deal closed in February 2001 the company warned that customers were cutting inventories, and the shares fell 51% that month. In July 2001 it wrote off about $50 billion of goodwill from its stock-financed acquisitions, then the largest write-off in corporate history, and cut its workforce from about 29,000 towards 5,300. From the February 2000 peak to November 2008 the stock lost practically all its value (-100% rounded in game terms; from about $153 to below $2 in actual prices). A one-for-eight reverse split in 2005 hid the low share price; a Connecticut securities suit over what executives knew in 2000 ended in acquittal in 2007.

The survivor reinvented itself around testing networks rather than supplying parts for them, beginning with the purchase of Acterna in 2005. Recoveries came and went (+45% in February 2011, lost again within the year). In August 2015 the company split in two: the optical components business became Lumentum and was distributed to shareholders, and JDS Uniphase renamed itself Viavi Solutions (the game follows Viavi, with prices adjusted for the spin-off). Under chief executive Oleg Khaykin from 2016, Viavi was a modest test-and-measurement company whose shares drifted between about 5 and 18 for a decade.

The last chapter is the AI data-centre build-out. With demand from data-centre and defence customers and the purchase of Spirent's high-speed Ethernet test business (October 2025), Viavi's sales grew 26%, 36% and 43% in successive quarters, and the shares roughly tripled between December 2025 and April 2026, with the biggest monthly gain in the company's history (+57% in April 2026). Even so, the price in 2026 was a fraction of the 2000 peak.

## Best and worst moments

- Peak: 2000-02 (price 600 in game terms) after +1,097% in the twelve months from 1999-02; big months 1999-10 (+47%), 1999-12 (+45%).
- Worst stretch: -100% (rounded) from 2000-02 to 2008-11; 2001-02 (-51%) the start of the crash, 2001-07 the $50 billion write-off, 2008-11 (-50%) the final low.
- Recoveries: 2002-11 (+52%), 2004-01 (+40%), 2009-04 (+42%), 2011-02 (+45%), none lasting.
- Turning points: 1995-05 entry into telecom; 1999-01 JDS Fitel merger; 2015-08 split into Viavi and Lumentum; 2025-10 to 2026-04 AI demand (+57% in 2026-04).

## Unexplained moves

- 1995-08 (+42%): no Uniphase filing or news found for August 1995 (EDGAR filings start in 1996). The fiscal year ended in June and results were probably reported in July or August; the first months with the newly acquired telecom unit may have driven it. Not confirmed.
- 1996-08 (+43%): a rebound after -27% in July 1996; fiscal 1996 results (10-K filed September 26) were probably published in late July or August. No specific August event found.
- 1999-12 (+45%): part of the optical-networking frenzy that continued from October and November 1999 (beats 1999-10, 1999-11); a two-for-one split approved in September took effect around the turn of the year. No December announcement found.
- 2002-11 (+52%): rebound of technology shares from the October 2002 low (market +6%, Cisco +33% the same month). JDS Uniphase's November 2002 8-Ks contain only investor presentations; the October 24 cost update is covered in 2002-10.
- 2005-09 (+41%): no news found besides the reverse-split proposal of September 23 (covered as a beat, without a move, since it is not a reason for a rise). The Acterna deal closed in August.
- 2008-11 (-50%): the financial-crisis sell-off (market -7%); the only November 8-K concerns an equity plan. The October results and the chief executive's resignation are covered in 2008-10.
- 2009-04 (+42%): coincides with the broad market rebound (market +9%); the April 29 results were weak (sales -27%), so the move is not attributed to them.
