# Lehman Brothers: fourteen years from spin-off to record profits to the largest bankruptcy in U.S. history

Span: 1994-05 to 2008-09 (bankruptcy).

## The story

Lehman Brothers came back to the stock market in May 1994, when American Express spun off the 144-year-old
bond trading and investment banking house it had bought ten years earlier. It was a small, lossmaking firm
(it lost $102 million in 1993), run by Richard Fuld, a bond trader who had spent his whole career there.
For two years it cut costs and went sideways.

The first near-death came in 1998. When Russia defaulted in August and the hedge fund Long-Term Capital
Management collapsed, investors fled every firm that traded risky bonds; Lehman's shares fell 45% in August
and another 28% in September. Lehman fought the rumours with disclosure (on 5 October it published its
hedge fund and emerging market exposures), the Federal Reserve cut rates three times, and by November the shares
were three quarters above their September low. Lehman reported a record 1998, and the episode built Fuld's reputation
as the man who saved the firm.

The decade that followed was the best in its history. Profits climbed through the technology boom and,
after 2001, through the housing boom: Lehman owned mortgage lenders (Aurora, BNC), packaged home loans into
securities, and bought commercial property. Net income went from $736 million in 1998 to $2.4 billion in
2004 and a record $4.2 billion in 2007; the shares rose about eighteenfold from the spin-off to the peak in
January 2007. To get there Lehman borrowed heavily: its assets grew to about 31 times its equity, funded
largely with short-term loans that had to be renewed every day.

When the mortgage market broke in 2007, Lehman closed its subprime lender but kept reporting profits while
others wrote down billions. After Bear Stearns fell in March 2008, the market's question was simply whether
Lehman's lenders would stay. Short-seller David Einhorn questioned its accounting in May; in June Lehman
reported a $2.8 billion loss, raised $6 billion and replaced its president and finance chief. A Korean
bank looked at buying it in August and walked away on 9 September; on 10 September Lehman announced a
$3.9 billion loss. Over the weekend of 13-14 September Barclays and Bank of America declined to buy it
without government support, the government refused to give it, and on 15 September Lehman filed for
bankruptcy with $639 billion of assets. The shares, $82 in January 2007, ended at 22 cents. The lesson: a
highly leveraged firm funded day to day can lose everything in months, however long its record of profits.

## Best and worst moments

- 1998-08: -45%, Russia's default and the flight from risky bonds.
- 1998-09: -28%, Russia losses, LTCM rescue, rumours about Wall Street firms.
- 1998-10 and 1998-11: +34% and +32%, Lehman's disclosure and three Fed rate cuts end the panic.
- 1999-01: +24%, record 1998 results.
- 2000-03: +34%, quarterly profit up 156% at the peak of the boom.
- 2007-01: the peak, about $82 (split-adjusted price in the game).
- 2008-03: -26%, Bear Stearns falls; Lehman is "next".
- 2008-06: -46%, first loss, $6 billion raised, management purge.
- 2008-09: -99%, bankruptcy.
- Best twelve months: +170% from 1999-08 to 2000-08. Worst stretch: -100% from 2007-01 to 2008-09.

## Unexplained moves

- 1999-10 (+26%, market +6%): no Lehman announcement found (the third-quarter results came on 23 September).
  The likely cause is Congress's progress that month on repealing the Glass-Steagall separation of banking
  and securities, which raised takeover hopes for independent investment banks, but no source ties Lehman's
  move to it. Checked: Lehman's 8-K filings for 1999 (EDGAR), Wikipedia (Lehman Brothers, Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act).
- 2000-08 (+29%, market +6%): no Lehman announcement found; the third-quarter results came on 20 September.
  It coincided with a wave of takeovers of Wall Street firms by larger banks in the summer of 2000, but the
  sources read here do not confirm a link. Checked: Lehman 8-K filings for 2000 (EDGAR), Wikipedia.
- 2000-12 (+36%, market flat): no Lehman announcement found (full-year results came on 4 January 2001).
  On 19 December the Federal Reserve said the risks were now weighted toward economic weakness, signalling
  rate cuts, which tends to lift brokerage shares; no source names this as the cause for Lehman.
  Checked: Fed statement of 19 December 2000, Lehman 8-K filings.
