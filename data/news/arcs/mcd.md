# McDonald's: two decades of global expansion, a near-collapse in 2002, then a turnaround that made it a crisis-proof stock

Span: 1979-12 (game start; data from 1975-01) to today.

## The story

At the start of the game McDonald's is the largest hamburger chain, with about 5,200 restaurants at the end of 1978 (720 of them abroad), record earnings every year and more than 500 openings a year. Most restaurants are run by franchisees who pay rent and fees, which makes McDonald's partly a property company. Burger King and Wendy's fight it in the 'burger wars', and experts claim the market is saturated, but McDonald's keeps growing: Chicken McNuggets (1981-83), the death of founder Ray Kroc (January 1984) without disruption, the 10,000th restaurant in 1988 and the opening in Moscow in January 1990. The crash of October 1987 (-16%) is a market event only.

The 1990s are the decade of international expansion: from about 3,600 restaurants abroad in 1991 to more than 11,000 in 1998, while the US business stalls. Too many new US restaurants cannibalise each other, and a string of menu flops (McLean Deluxe, Arch Deluxe) and the confused 55-cent Big Mac promotion of 1997 anger franchisees. The shares lag the market in 1996-97, then gain about 60% in 1998 on a turnaround plan ('Made for You' kitchens, a new CEO, Jack Greenberg) and peak at the end of 1999.

Then everything goes wrong at once. Made for You slows service, sales at existing restaurants fall in 2000 and 2001, the brand becomes a target of anti-globalisation protests and obesity lawsuits, and profits fall in seven of eight quarters. The dot-com rotation (February 2000, -15%, reversed in March, +18%) is a sideshow. In September 2002 another profit warning sends the shares down 26% in a month; Greenberg leaves in December and McDonald's posts its first quarterly loss. From the end of 1999 to February 2003 the shares lose about 70%.

Jim Cantalupo, back from retirement, stops the empire-building: fewer new restaurants, lower capital spending, better food and service at existing ones ('Plan to Win', 'I'm lovin' it'). The shares jump 18% in April 2003 on the plan and 18% in June on the first strong sales, and more than double in a year. Cantalupo dies suddenly in April 2004 and his successor Charlie Bell falls ill with cancer within weeks, but Jim Skinner keeps the course. Under Skinner McDonald's sheds Chipotle, quadruples its dividend and thrives in the 2008 recession, one of the few big stocks to rise that year. After a weak patch (2013-14) Steve Easterbrook's all-day breakfast turnaround (October 2015, +14%) and Chris Kempczinski's digital and value push carry the shares to records; the pandemic (March 2020) and the exit from Russia (2022) are absorbed easily, and the value positioning pays off in the inflation of 2022 (October 2022, +18%).

The shares peaked in February 2026 and have since lost about a third, with US sales sluggish; in September 2026 (-12%) McDonald's presented its 'NEXT' strategy with billions of dollars of support for franchisees.

## Best and worst moments

- 1987-10: -16% in the crash.
- 1998: +61% for the year on the 'Made for You' turnaround hopes (February 1998 +16%, unexplained).
- 1998-08: -16% in the Russian crisis sell-off.
- 2000-02: -15% (old-economy sell-off) and 2000-03: +18% (blue-chip rebound).
- Peak of the first era: November-December 1999.
- 2002-09: -26%, profit warning; worst stretch -70% from November 1999 to February 2003.
- 2003-04: +18% (Cantalupo's plan) and 2003-06: +18% (best US sales in four years); best twelve months +108% from February 2003 to February 2004.
- 2008: shares up while the S&P 500 lost more than a third.
- 2015-10: +14%, all-day breakfast quarter.
- 2022-10: +18%, strong sales despite inflation.
- Record: February 2026 (341 dollars month-end); 2026-09: -12% after the 'NEXT' plan.

## Unexplained moves

- 1975-02 (+19%), 1975-04 (+24%), 1975-07 (-15%): before the game starts (chart history only). Not researched beyond checking that the archive.org annual-report collection has no 1975 report.
- 1998-02 (+16%, market +7%): about half the move is the market. In February 1998 McDonald's bought a minority stake in Chipotle, too small to move the shares; the turnaround measures ('Made for You', menu changes) were announced in March. Checked the 1997 annual report (letter of 15 March 1998), the company history and web searches (rate-limited); no specific cause found.
