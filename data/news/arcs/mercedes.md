# Daimler-Benz / DaimlerChrysler / Daimler / Mercedes-Benz Group: forty-five years of empire building and retreat, ending where it began, as a maker of expensive cars

Span: 1981-03 to today.

## The story

When players can first buy it in March 1981, Daimler-Benz is the most solid company in Germany: the maker of Mercedes cars and one of the world's biggest truck makers, owned in large part by the Deutsche Bank, Kuwait (about 14% since 1974) and the Mercedes-Automobil-Holding. The early 1980s are good years: the S-Class (1979) and the compact 190 (1982) sell well, and in 1985 foreign investors discover German shares. Daimler is their favourite, and the Plaza Accord's falling dollar adds to the gain in dollar terms. Between April 1985 and April 1986 the share triples in dollars (+202%), the best twelve months in its history in the game.

The money is spent on diversification. In 1985 Daimler buys MTU, Dornier and AEG, and in 1989 MBB; Edzard Reuter, chairman from 1987 to 1995, wants an "integrated technology group" of cars, aircraft, defence, electrical goods and services. The market goes along in good moments (the crash rebound of February 1988, the "Germany boom" after the fall of the Wall in December 1989), but the businesses do not fit. The end of the Cold War hurts defence, the weak dollar of the 1990s ruins the aircraft business, and the 1992-93 recession hits cars. The New York listing of October 1993 forces American accounting, and the losses become visible. Der Spiegel later calls the Reuter era the greatest destruction of capital in German peacetime history: worth 47.6 billion marks in 1987, the company is worth 35.2 billion in 1995, after 6 billion of new equity.

Jürgen Schrempp, who took over in May 1995, first cleans up (1995 loss of 5.7 billion marks under US rules, Fokker cut loose in January 1996, AEG dissolved) and the share recovers strongly. Then he builds his own empire: the "merger of equals" with Chrysler (agreed May 1998, trading from November 1998), stakes in Mitsubishi Motors and Hyundai. Chrysler soon loses money (record loss in Q3 2000, 26,000 job cuts in 2001), the Asian stakes are abandoned in 2004-05, and the share drifts far below its 1998 level, with a -32% month after September 11. The market celebrates Schrempp's resignation in July 2005 (+20%). His successor Dieter Zetsche sells 80.1% of Chrysler to Cerberus in May 2007, and the company becomes Daimler AG in October 2007, just before the financial crisis.

The crisis is the worst stretch (-79% from October 2007 to February 2009, -32% in October 2008 alone). Abu Dhabi's Aabar buys 9.1% in March 2009, the last ties to Chrysler are cut in April 2009 (+42%, the best month), and the post-crisis boom in Chinese luxury demand lifts German car makers (2010). The 2010s are volatile but flat: the euro crisis (August 2011), the diesel scandal that starts at Volkswagen in 2015 and reaches Mercedes in 2017, Chinese shareholders (Geely's founder 2018, BAIC 2019), trade wars and a string of profit warnings in 2018-19. Ola Källenius takes over in May 2019; COVID crashes the share in March 2020 and the vaccine news of November 2020 sends it soaring.

In December 2021 the trucks are spun off as Daimler Truck, and in February 2022 the company renames itself Mercedes-Benz Group: a pure luxury car maker again, now promising "fewer cars, more luxury". Record profits and buybacks hold the share up until Chinese demand for German luxury cars collapses (profit warning September 2024). In 2026 the war with Iran (March), BMW's profit warning (June) and a collapse of Chinese sales plus a US bill aimed at Chinese-owned car makers (September) push the share to new lows. Over the whole game the share gains only about 330% in dollars, far behind the market.

## Best and worst moments

- Best twelve months: April 1985 to April 1986, +202% (foreign buying, Plaza Accord, AEG). Big months 1985-06 +27%, 1985-10 +27%.
- Crash: 1987-10 -23% and 1987-11 -18%; rebound 1988-02 +27%.
- Reunification boom: 1989-12 +34%; Gulf crisis 1990-08 -16% and 1990-09 -18%.
- Turning point: 1995-07 (the bill for the Reuter years), followed by the Schrempp cleanup and the 1996-98 recovery.
- Chrysler: 1998-05 merger agreed, 1998-11 +18% at the first trading of DaimlerChrysler; 2001-09 -32%; 2005-07 +20% when Schrempp quit; 2007-05 Chrysler sold.
- Worst stretch: -79% from 2007-10 to 2009-02 (2008-10 -32%); best month 2009-04 +42%.
- 2010-09 +30% (car boom plus a rising euro), 2011-08 -26% (euro crisis).
- 2020-03 -27% (COVID), 2020-11 +30% (vaccine).
- 2026: -13% (March), -18% (June), -15% (September) as China sales collapse.

## Unexplained moves

- 2009-07: +28% (market +7%). Checked Google News for Daimler results and news in July 2009 (only a sales decline of 11% in July, reported in August), the Mercedes chronicle for 2009 and the BMW annual report 2009, which describes a general recovery of stock markets from the March low. BMW (+23%) and Siemens (+16%) rose strongly the same month, so it looks like a broad rally in cyclical German shares, but no Daimler-specific cause was found.
