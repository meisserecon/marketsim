# Iran and the Middle East: from the revolution's first winter to a war that killed its leader

Span: 1979-12 to today (sources carry to July 2026).

## The story

The game opens in the first winter of the Islamic Republic. In December 1979 Iranians voted
for a constitution that put a senior cleric at the head of the state, while students held
Americans hostage in the US embassy, and the United States had stopped buying Iranian oil and
frozen Iranian money. In the same weeks Soviet troops entered Afghanistan and gold had its
wildest months in the game. The hostages came home after 444 days, but the break between
Washington and Tehran never healed. Inside Iran the revolution turned on its own: the first
president was deposed in June 1981, bombs killed much of the leadership, and thousands of
opponents were arrested or executed.

From September 1980 Iran fought the war Iraq had started, the "imposed war" or "sacred
defence" in Iranian memory. Iran lost Khorramshahr in 1980 and retook it in May 1982, then
carried the war into Iraq for six more years: human-wave offensives by young volunteers,
Iraqi poison gas against soldiers and later against the Kurds of Halabja, missiles on Tehran,
and a tanker war that drew the US Navy into the Gulf. It ended in July 1988 with the
shooting-down of Iran Air 655 (290 dead) and Khomeini's acceptance of a ceasefire he called
"more deadly than poison". Estimates of the dead run to around half a million. In the weeks
after the ceasefire, thousands of political prisoners were executed in secret; Amnesty
International's first full account came only in 1990, and the numbers (2,800 to 5,000 in
most estimates, far higher by some) are still disputed. Khomeini died in June 1989;
Khamenei succeeded him and ruled for almost 37 years. Rafsanjani's reconstruction and
Khatami's reform years (1997 to 2005) were the most hopeful stretch for many Iranians,
checked by the crushing of the 1999 student protests. Two earthquakes, Manjil-Rudbar in 1990
and Bam in 2003, killed tens of thousands.

Around Iran, the region's turns moved the world and sometimes the markets. Lebanon (1982-83)
brought massacre, the Beirut bombings and the birth of Hezbollah. Iraq's invasion of Kuwait
in August 1990 is the theme's clearest market shock (S&P 500 -9% that month), and the start
of the air war in January 1991 its clearest relief rally. Oslo (1993) raised hopes that
Rabin's murder (1995) dimmed. 9/11 (S&P 500 -8% in September 2001) led to Afghanistan and
Iraq; Baghdad's fall in April 2003 brought another relief rally (+8%). The Arab Spring of
2011, Syria's war and the rise of ISIS in 2014 followed.

The nuclear dispute runs from the exiles' revelation of Natanz in August 2002 through UN
sanctions (2006-2010), the EU oil embargo and the rial's collapse in 2012, Rouhani's victory in
2013 and the 2015 deal, to Trump's withdrawal in May 2018. Companies in the game touch it
directly: fines for sanctions breaches at UBS (2004), Credit Suisse (2009) and Schlumberger
(2015), the Nokia Siemens monitoring-equipment report during the 2009 protests, Boeing's
16.6 billion dollar Iran Air order (2016) and its loss in 2018, Siemens' and Daimler's retreat
the same year. The street has its own milestones: the Green Movement of 2009, the petrol
protests of November 2019, the downing of Flight 752 in January 2020 (many of its victims
Iranian-Canadians), "Woman, Life, Freedom" after Mahsa Amini's death in September 2022.

After 7 October 2023 the shadow war became open war: Iran's first direct strike on Israel in
April 2024, the gutting of Hezbollah and the fall of Assad in late 2024, twelve days of war
in June 2025. The uprising of late December 2025 was met on 8 and 9 January 2026 with the
bloodiest crackdown in the Islamic Republic's history (tolls disputed, from about 3,000 by
the government's count to well over 7,000 confirmed by rights groups and higher estimates
still). On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel went to war with Iran; Khamenei was
killed, Hormuz was closed and oil passed 100 dollars. A Pakistani-brokered ceasefire in April
lifted markets (S&P 500 +10%), a June framework deal frayed and the truce collapsed in July.
The arc ends there: a new Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, a ruined economy and a society
scarred by massacre and war. For a family with Iranian roots, much of this is family history;
the beats try to say what was known at the time and to give the human cost its place next to
the market moves.

## Best and worst moments

Market moves from movers.md that this theme explains:

- 1979-12, gold +16% and 1980-01, gold +48%: hostages in Tehran, the Soviet entry into Afghanistan and the Carter Doctrine, on top of inflation. Gold's peak month in the game.
- 1990-08, S&P 500 -9%: Iraq invades Kuwait; the theme's worst market month and the main reason 1990 was a down year for shares.
- 1991-01, S&P 500 +4% (and 1991-02, +7%): Desert Storm begins, oil falls by a third in a day, stocks leap.
- 2001-09, S&P 500 -8%: the September 11 attacks; Dow -7.1% on reopening, -14% that week.
- 2003-04, S&P 500 +8%: Baghdad falls quickly, the war premium fades.
- 2026-03, S&P 500 -5% (Siemens -19%, BMW -15%): war with Iran, Hormuz closed, oil above 100 dollars; the S&P bottomed on 30 March.
- 2026-04, S&P 500 +10%: Pakistan-brokered ceasefire; the relief rally (with AI stocks also driving the month).

Turning points without a market move: the hostage release (1981-01), Khorramshahr retaken
(1982-05), the ceasefire (1988-07), Khomeini's death (1989-06), Khatami (1997-05), the
nuclear deal (2015-07) and its collapse (2018-05), Mahsa Amini (2022-09), Khamenei's death
(2026-02).

## Unexplained moves

- 1980-03 (S&P 500 -10%), 1980-11 (+10%), 1982-08, 1982-10, 1984-08, 1987-01, 1987-10, 1998-08, 2008-10 and the other large S&P months: checked against the theme; no Middle East event drives them (credit controls, rates, elections, the 1987 crash, LTCM, the financial crisis). The 1987 tanker war and Iran's Gulf clashes ran through October 1987 but are not the cause of the crash.
- 2020-03 (S&P 500 -13%, Schlumberger -50%): the Saudi-Russian price war belongs to the oil arc and Covid to the pandemic; Iran's early Covid outbreak and the January 2020 Soleimani crisis did not move the market.
- 2026-01 (Schlumberger +26%, ExxonMobil +18%): the Iranian uprising and massacre fell in this month, but no source links the oil-stock rise to Iran; left to the oil arc.
- 2026-09 (Schlumberger -19%): sources mention a Houthi crisis at Bab al-Mandab in September 2026, but nothing confirms it as the cause; not used.
- Calendar years: only 1990 (Gulf crisis) is a year this theme explains. Note that the calendar-year labels in movers.md appear shifted by one year against the monthly figures (for example the -40% listed for 2009 matches 2008); worth checking in movers.ts.
