# Microsoft: the toll on every PC, a lost decade after the antitrust case, and a second life in the cloud and AI

Span: 1986-03 to today.

## The story

Microsoft came to the market in March 1986 as the company whose MS-DOS ran on the IBM PC and every copy of it. It was small (under 200 million dollars of sales) but it collected a fee on nearly every personal computer sold, and the shares rose almost fivefold in eighteen months, making Bill Gates a billionaire at 31. The partnership with IBM on OS/2 was still the official future in 1987; Windows, ridiculed and sued over by Apple in 1988, became the real one. Windows 3.0 in May 1990 was the breakthrough, Microsoft passed a billion dollars in sales that year, and Windows 3.1 and the court ruling against Apple in April 1992 secured the franchise.

The 1990s were the decade of Windows and Office. Windows 95 (August 1995) was a cultural event; Office 97 drove 45% sales growth; Windows was on more than nine in ten PCs. The same dominance drew the antitrust authorities: a first settlement in 1994, then the big case filed in May 1998 over the bundling of Internet Explorer. Investors ignored it. The shares rose another 175% between the lawsuit and December 1999, when Microsoft set a record for stock market value that stood until Apple passed it in 2012.

December 1999 was the top for almost seventeen years. Gates handed the CEO job to Steve Ballmer in January 2000; in April Judge Jackson ruled that Microsoft had broken the law, the shares fell 15% in a day and 34% in the month as the dot-com bubble burst, and in June the court ordered a breakup. The appeals court threw the breakup out in June 2001 and the case ended in a settlement, but the business had changed: Microsoft started paying dividends (2003), handed back cash on a huge scale (the 3-dollar special dividend of 2004), missed search (the failed Yahoo bid of 2008), phones and tablets, and watched Apple overtake it in market value in May 2010. From December 1999 to February 2009 the shares lost 72%.

Satya Nadella, named CEO in February 2014, turned Microsoft into a cloud company. Azure and Office 365 started to show in the numbers in 2015, LinkedIn (2016) and Activision Blizzard (2022-23) were bought, and Microsoft passed a trillion dollars in value in April 2019. The early bet on OpenAI (2019, extended with a multibillion-dollar investment in January 2023) made Microsoft the first big winner of the generative AI boom. By 2025 Azure alone brought in more than 75 billion dollars a year.

In 2026 the AI story cut both ways. Investors feared that AI could make business software obsolete and that the hundreds of billions spent on data centres would not pay off; the shares fell about 35% from the October 2025 peak by March 2026. Strong results in July 2026, with Azure above 100 billion dollars a year, brought them back near the peak by September.

## Best and worst moments

- 1986-03: listing at 21 dollars; 1986-09 to 1987-09: best twelve months, +369%.
- 1987-10: -25% in the crash.
- 1990-05: +26% on the launch of Windows 3.0.
- 1995-08: Windows 95.
- 1997-04: +33% on Office 97 sales.
- 1999-12: +28%, the peak of the first era.
- 2000-04: -34% after the antitrust ruling and the start of the dot-com crash.
- 1999-12 to 2009-02: worst stretch, -72%.
- 2001-01: +41% rebound after the surprise rate cut.
- 2014-02: Nadella becomes CEO, start of the second act.
- 2026-01 to 2026-03: -11%, -9%, -6% on AI-disruption and spending fears; 2026-07: +25% recovery.

## Unexplained moves

- 1986-10: +37% and 1986-11: +28% (market +5% and +2%). Checked Microsoft's own history for 1986 (product launches only: Works for the Mac, the desktop publishing alliance with Aldus and HP) and the IPO coverage; no financial news for these months was found. Probably the strong quarterly results expected in October and growing enthusiasm for the young stock, but not confirmed.
- 1987-01: +52% (market +13%). Part of the January 1987 market boom; Microsoft's history page for 1987 lists no event in January. Quarterly results would normally fall in the month, but no source with the figures or date was found.
- 1987-08: +26% (market +3%). Checked Microsoft's 1987 history (purchase of Forethought, maker of PowerPoint, on 30 July; Bookshelf and Works in September); nothing that explains a 26% rise.
- 1991-01: +30% (market +4%). Checked Microsoft's 1991 history (strong Windows 3.0 application sales reported in March); no January event found. Pre-1993 filings are not on EDGAR.
- 1998-06: +28% (market +4%). Windows 98 went on sale on 25 June in more than 40 countries (Microsoft history, 1998); whether its launch, or court developments in the antitrust case that month, drove the rise could not be confirmed in the sources read.
- 1999-12: +28% (market +6%). The height of the technology boom; Microsoft reached its all-time high for the first era this month. No company-specific event found in the sources read.
- 2000-06: +28% (market +2%). The breakup order of 7 June is a beat, but the shares rose in the month; the cause of the rise (relief that the order would be appealed, or other news) could not be confirmed.
