# Nestlé: four decades of steady compounding from Vevey, then a long stall after 2021

Span: 1979-12 to today.

## The story

In December 1979 Nestlé is already the largest food company in the world: Nescafé, milk powder, baby food, chocolate and Maggi, sold almost everywhere, plus a stake in L'Oréal and the American eye-care firm Alcon. It also carries a moral burden: since 1977 activists have boycotted it for pushing baby milk on mothers in poor countries. The WHO code of 1981 and Nestlé's agreement to follow it in 1984 close the first phase of the boycott, but the theme never goes away, and it comes back as late as the formula recall of January 2026.

Helmut Maucher, a German who becomes chief executive in November 1981, turns Nestlé into a buyer. Carnation (1984, $3 billion: evaporated milk, Coffee-Mate, Friskies) and Rowntree (1988, £2.55 billion: Kit Kat, After Eight, Smarties) make it a force in America and in confectionery; Perrier (1992) and Sanpellegrino (1997) make it the leader in bottled water. Nespresso, launched quietly in 1986, takes a decade to catch on and then becomes one of the group's crown jewels. The share does what a great consumer company's share should: our series rises more than fortyfold in dollars over the game.

Two things make the 1980s price chart look stranger than the business. First the franc: the game shows dollar prices, and the dollar's swings against the franc (1.85 francs in March 1980, 2.86 in February 1985, 1.27 at the end of 1987) add or subtract ten percent in single months, as in March and April 1980 and July 1985. Second the share classes. Until November 1988 foreigners could only buy the bearer share, which traded at about twice the price of the registered share reserved for Swiss holders, and our series follows the bearer share. When the board opened the register to foreigners on 17 November 1988, the bearer premium collapsed: the bearer share fell 23% in francs in a month while the registered share rose 37%. Combined with the October 1987 crash, that gives the worst stretch of the game, -42% from September 1987 to May 1989, without anything going wrong in the business. The classes were unified in 1993.

Under Peter Brabeck (chief executive from 1997, also chairman from 2005) and Paul Bulcke (from 2008), Nestlé grows into "nutrition, health and wellness": Ralston Purina (2001) makes it the world leader in pet food, Gerber (2007, bought from Novartis) in baby food, and the sale of Alcon to Novartis in 2008-2010 turns a 1977 purchase into a large gain. The franc again matters: the cap of September 2011 cost the share 11% in dollars in one month while it stood still in francs, and the end of the cap in January 2015 made it fall in francs and rise in dollars.

Mark Schneider (2017-2024) brings activist pressure (Third Point, 2017), buybacks, Starbucks coffee and a push into health science; the share peaks in December 2021. Then inflation turns the story: in 2022 and 2023 almost all growth comes from price increases, volumes stall, and the share falls about 40% from its peak to the end of 2024. Schneider is replaced by Laurent Freixe in 2024, Freixe is fired a year later over an undisclosed relationship, and Philipp Navratil, the former head of Nespresso, cuts 16,000 jobs and puts the water business into a joint venture. February 2026 brings the best month since 1997, but at the end of the game the share is still about a third below its 2021 high.

## Best and worst moments

- Best twelve months: +101% from 1984-12 to 1985-12, the Swiss boom plus the falling dollar; Nestlé at record highs on 30 December 1985.
- 1988-11: -20%, the register opening; the bearer premium vanishes (the defining price event).
- 1987-09 to 1989-05: -42%, the worst stretch (crash of 1987-10, -19%, then the register opening).
- 1997-09: +20%, the best single month, cause not found.
- 1999-01: -16% on weak sales.
- 2011-09: -11% in dollars with the share flat in francs (franc cap).
- 2021-12: all-time high (139 in game dollars).
- 2024-02 (-9%) to 2024-12: the stall; volumes flat after the inflation years, low at the end of 2024.
- 2025-02: +13% and 2026-02: +15%, relief rallies on results under Freixe and Navratil.

## Unexplained moves

- 1982-12 (+13%): the franc rose 5.5% against the dollar, which explains less than half; the rest (+7% in francs) has no identified cause. Checked: existing 1982 news items (the French UFC boycott call of 30 November is negative), Wikipedia, the NZZ notes for 1982 (Nestlé lines unreadable in December).
- 1986-08 (+17%, +15% in francs) and 1986-11 (+14%, +9% in francs): no company news found. Checked: Wikipedia, nestle.com history, NZZ notes 1986 (the bearer share rose from 7,425 in July to 9,610 francs in November), existing 1986 news items. The NZZ issues of 30 August and 29 November 1986 were not read page by page.
- 1997-09 (+20%, +17% in francs): the Swiss market rebounded after an August sell-off (NZZ index chart, 1 October 1997, p. 35: SMI from about 5,300 in late August to 5,674 on 30 September), but that explains less than half. The NZZ market commentary of 1 October does not mention Nestlé. Half-year results may have been published in September but were not confirmed.
