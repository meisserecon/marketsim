# Netscape Communications: the browser that started the internet boom, beaten by Microsoft, sold to AOL

Span: 1995-09 to 1999-02 (taken over by America Online on 17 March 1999; exit beat 1999-03).

## The story

Netscape was founded in April 1994 by Jim Clark, the founder of Silicon Graphics, and Marc Andreessen, who had co-written the Mosaic browser at the University of Illinois. Its Navigator browser, released in October 1994, took most of the market within months, and its public offering of 9 August 1995 (priced at $28, closing at $58.25; beat 35251842, moved here from tech) was the starting gun of the dot-com era. In the game the share enters in August 1995 at that first-day close, by the curator's decision of 17 October 2026, because no 31 August close could be sourced (see manual/netscape.csv); the first month-end close is September's. Revenue nearly doubled every quarter in 1995 ($11.9 million, $20.8 million, $40.6 million), Navigator 2.0 added Java, and by November the company was worth about $4 billion; the share peaked on 5 December 1995.

Two days later Bill Gates announced that Microsoft would embrace the internet and give its own browser away. From then on Netscape's share price was a running vote on whether it could survive Microsoft. It fell by 38% in February 1996 (also the end of the lock-up for employees' shares), rallied on strong quarters in spring 1996, fell again in the technology sell-off of July 1996, and slid through the browser war of 1996/1997: Internet Explorer 3.0 in August 1996, built into Windows, given to computer makers and to AOL and CompuServe. Netscape kept growing revenue (1996: $346 million, 1997: $534 million) by selling server software to companies, but analysts turned against it in January 1997, and in January 1998 it announced its first loss, layoffs, and that Navigator would be free and its source code public (the Mozilla project, code released on 31 March 1998).

In spring 1998 investors rediscovered Netscape as a web portal: Netcenter, the default start page of millions of browsers. The Justice Department sued Microsoft in May 1998, largely over its treatment of Netscape (msft 1c6b3e28). On 24 November 1998 America Online agreed to buy Netscape for 0.45 AOL shares per share, about $4.2 billion; Sun Microsystems took on the business software in a parallel alliance. Because AOL's shares then soared (S&P 500 inclusion in December 1998, the internet frenzy of early 1999), the deal was worth about $10 billion when it closed on 17 March 1999. AOL split its shares 2 for 1 on 22 February 1999, so holders actually received 0.90 AOL share per Netscape share (AOL 8-K of 17 March 1999).

A player who bought at the September 1995 listing and held to the end converts at +148% into AOL shares, but only after an 80% fall from January 1996 to January 1998. Netscape is in the game as the first internet star and the clearest case of a company crushed by a dominant competitor.

## Best and worst moments

- Listing: 1995-09 at 31.25 (split-adjusted).
- Best months: 1998-11 (+73%), the AOL deal; 1998-12 (+64%), riding AOL's S&P 500 inclusion; 1995-11 (+57%), split and Goldman upgrade; 1998-04 (+50%), Netcenter; 1996-04 (+48%), first-quarter results.
- Worst months: 1997-02 (-48%, December to February), the analysts' turn; 1996-02 (-38%), Microsoft's free browser and the lock-up; 1996-07 (-37%), technology sell-off; 1998-08 (-37%), the August 1998 market fall; 1998-01 (-34%), first loss.
- Worst stretch: -80% from 1996-01 to 1998-01. Best twelve months: +360% from 1998-01 to 1999-01.
- Over its life: +148% from 1995-09 to 1999-02.
