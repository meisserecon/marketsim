# Novartis: the Basel merger that slimmed itself into a pure medicines company

Span: 1996-11 to today.

## The story

Novartis is born of the largest merger of its time: Sandoz and Ciba-Geigy, Basel neighbours and rivals for a century, announce it on 7 March 1996, and the new company begins operations on 20 December 1996, with Ciba's Alex Krauer as chairman and Sandoz's Daniel Vasella as chief executive. In the game Sandoz holders become Novartis holders at the end of November 1996. At first it is a life-sciences conglomerate: medicines, generics, farm chemicals and seeds, Gerber baby food.

The Vasella years (1996-2013) are about building. The farm business goes into Syngenta with AstraZeneca's in November 2000. Glivec, approved in record time in May 2001, becomes the symbol of targeted cancer drugs and of Novartis's science, and of its pricing (from $30,000 a year in 2001 to over $90,000 by 2012). Generics are gathered under the old Sandoz name in 2003 and doubled with Hexal and Eon Labs in 2005; Chiron follows in 2006. The biggest bet is Alcon, bought from Nestlé in steps from 2008 to 2011 for about $60 billion in all. The share does not keep up: the worst stretch is -40% from October 2006 to the crisis low of February 2009, and Vasella's departure in 2013 with a package of about $78 million feeds the Swiss vote against "rip-off salaries" that March.

Under Joseph Jimenez (2010-2018) and Vas Narasimhan (from 2018) Novartis undoes much of what was built. Vaccines and consumer health go to GSK (2014-2018), Alcon is spun off in April 2019, the stake of about a third in Roche is given up in 2021, and Sandoz, the generics arm, becomes independent again on 4 October 2023, one Sandoz share for every five Novartis shares. What is left is a pure patented-medicines company, buying new drugs with the cash: AveXis (2018), The Medicines Company (2019), MorphoSys (2024), Avidity (2025).

As for all Swiss shares in the game, the franc matters: in January 2009 half of the 16% fall in dollars was the franc; the franc cap of September 2011 made Novartis rise 8% in francs and fall 4% in dollars; the end of the cap in January 2015 did the opposite. The spin-offs do not show as drops, because the price series is adjusted. In September 2026 Novartis stands about 14% below its all-time high of February 2026 and at about six times its starting price in dollars.

## Best and worst moments

- 1996-11/12: merger completed and Novartis listed.
- 1997-06: +18% in the Swiss boom; best twelve months +60% from 1997-02 to 1998-02.
- 2001-05: Glivec approved.
- 2006-10 to 2009-02: -40%, the worst stretch; 2009-01 (-16%) and 2009-02 (-12%) in the financial crisis.
- 2019-04: Alcon spun off; 2023-10: Sandoz spun off.
- 2020-11: +16% on the vaccine rally.
- 2026-02: +14% to the all-time high, then -11% in 2026-03.

## Unexplained moves

- 2000-01 (-16%, -13% in francs): no Novartis-specific news found. Swiss blue chips fell in January 2000 (a Credit Suisse Swiss blue-chip fund was down 6.2% for the year on 31 January, NZZ 1 February 2000, fund table p. 16), and investors favoured technology shares at the time, but that does not explain the full fall. Checked: NZZ of 1 February 2000 (text layer), Wikipedia.
- 2002-02 (+11%, +10% in francs): no cause found. Checked: Wikipedia, Novartis history pages. No NZZ scans exist after 2001.
- 2016-01 (-11%, -10% in francs, market -5%): partly the market; no Novartis-specific cause confirmed (fourth-quarter results are usually published in late January but were not read).
- 2023-04 (+11%, +9% in francs): first-quarter results month; no source read confirmed what was reported.
- 2026-02 (+14%): the fourth-quarter results of 4 February look weak (sales -1% at constant currencies, net income -15%); Nestlé rose 15% the same month, so a move into Swiss defensive shares is possible but not confirmed.
- 2026-03 (-11%): the franc fell about 4% against the dollar and the S&P 500 5%; the rest (-7% in francs) has no identified cause.
