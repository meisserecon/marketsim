# Nvidia: from a games-chip start-up through two crashes to the most valuable company in the world on the AI boom

Span: 1999-01 to today.

## The story

Nvidia came to the market in January 1999 as one of the survivors of the 1990s graphics-chip wars: a company that designed 3D chips for PC games, had them made by others, and had nearly run out of money in 1997 before its RIVA chips saved it. The GeForce 256 (announced August 1999), which Nvidia marketed as the first "GPU", made it the leader in PC graphics, and the contract for the Xbox graphics in March 2000 made it a major supplier. The shares rose more than eightfold in the twelve months to September 2000, and Nvidia bought the assets of its fallen rival 3dfx in December 2000. While the dot-com bust crushed other technology stocks, Nvidia quadrupled in 2001 and joined the S&P 500 in place of Enron.

The first collapse came in 2002. An SEC inquiry led to a restatement of three years of accounts (April 2002), Microsoft took a pricing dispute over Xbox chips to arbitration, and ATI's Radeon 9700 overtook Nvidia's products while its own GeForce FX arrived late and disappointed. From December 2001 to September 2002 the shares fell 87%. They recovered in jumps (+39% and +44% in October and November 2002, +83% in May 2003) and rose more than tenfold to the end of 2007, when Forbes named Nvidia its company of the year. Then the 2008 crisis, a charge for faulty notebook chips (July 2008, -39%) and the end of its chipset business in a legal fight with Intel took the shares down by three quarters.

The quiet decision that changed everything came in 2007: CUDA, software that let researchers use graphics chips for general calculations. For years it hardly showed. Nvidia spent 2011-2015 trying mobile processors (Tegra) and cars, and the shares went sideways. From 2016 deep learning, which runs best on GPUs, began to show in the numbers: the shares more than tripled in 2016 on the Pascal chips and data-centre demand. A crypto-mining boom and bust in 2018 halved them, and the attempt to buy Arm (2020-22) failed, but the data-centre business kept growing.

ChatGPT, launched in November 2022 and trained on Nvidia chips in Microsoft's data centres, set off the AI boom. In May 2023 Nvidia forecast quarterly revenue of 11 billion dollars, half more than expected, and became the defining stock of the era: a trillion dollars in 2023, two trillion in March 2024, the world's most valuable company in June 2024, four trillion in July 2025 and five trillion in October 2025. The doubts were sharp but short: DeepSeek's cheap model cost Nvidia 600 billion dollars of value in a day in January 2025, and US export rules repeatedly cut off its China business. By August 2026 revenue had doubled again to 96 billion dollars a quarter.

Nvidia's history in the game is one of extreme moves: a dozen months with moves of about 40% or more, a worst stretch of -87%, and a gain of more than 5,000 times from the IPO price.

## Best and worst moments

- 1999-09 to 2000-09: best twelve months, +751%; 1999-11 +56% as the GeForce 256 sold, 2000-02 +73% on record results in the technology mania, 2000-03 +32% with the Xbox contract.
- 2001-01: +58% after the surprise rate cut.
- 2001-12 to 2002-09: worst stretch, -87% (restatement, Xbox dispute, ATI's lead); 2002-06: -49%.
- 2002-10 and 2002-11: +39% and +44% from the market bottom and with the GeForce FX; 2003-05: +83%.
- 2008-07: -39% on the faulty-chip charge; 2008 as a whole -76%.
- 2011-01: +55% on the 1.5 billion dollar Intel settlement.
- 2016-05 and 2016-11: +32% and +30% as deep learning drove growth.
- 2018-11: -22% in the crypto hangover.
- 2023-05: +36% on the 11 billion dollar forecast; the start of the AI boom in the price.
- 2024-06: most valuable company in the world; 2025-10: first company worth five trillion dollars.
- 2025-01: DeepSeek, the largest one-day loss of market value in US history.

## Unexplained moves

- 1999-08: +40% (market -1%). The GeForce 256 was announced on 31 August, the last day of the month, so it cannot explain the rise; the 10-Q for the quarter to July 1999 was not checked in detail. Possibly anticipation of the new chip or strong quarterly results, not confirmed.
- 2001-03: +45% (market -6%). Checked Nvidia's filings around the period (10-K for fiscal 2001 filed April 2001, the 3dfx closing in April 2001); no event in March 2001 found in the sources read. Annual results were probably published in February, not March.
- 2001-10: +56% (market +2%). The Xbox launch (15 November) and the strong growth reported in the 10-Q for the quarter to October 2001 are the likely background, but no dated October event was found.
- 2002-06: -49% (market -7%). Checked the 10-Q filed 10 June 2002 (no new warning found), the April restatement and Xbox arbitration (beat 2002-04) and the SEC enforcement letter of August 2002; the cause of the June fall (beyond the falling market and the earlier accounting cloud) was not found.
- 2009-12: +43% (market +2%). Checked the results of 5 November 2009 (revenue up 16% from the previous quarter) and the Wikipedia history; no December 2009 event found.
