# Priceline / Booking Holdings: the name-your-price bubble stock that fell 99% and was reborn through Booking.com

Span: 1999-03 to today.

## The story

Jay Walker's Priceline.com let travellers bid for airline seats and hotel rooms that airlines and hotels could not sell, with William Shatner in the advertising. It listed on March 30, 1999 (10 million shares at $16, raising $160 million) as one of the hottest offerings of the internet boom; the shares almost doubled again in April 1999, and Walker, with a 35% stake, became a multi-billionaire on paper. A player buying in 1999 was paying for a patented idea and rapid revenue growth, not profits.

The idea was then stretched to everything: groceries and petrol through the licensee WebHouse Club, cars, mortgages, long-distance calls, licences abroad. When the market turned in 2000 the house of cards fell in four months. On September 27, 2000 Priceline warned that third-quarter revenue would miss; WebHouse Club shut down in October, costing Priceline a $189 million write-off; Walker's own company ran out of money; in December Priceline abandoned its new ventures. The shares fell 56%, 52%, 57% and 48% in four consecutive months, a total of about -97%, and -99% from the April 1999 high.

What survived was the travel business, and it was better than its share price. Revenue had kept growing (third quarter 2000: $341 million, +124%), Delta restructured its stake, Li Ka-shing's Hutchison and Cheung Kong bought in (June 2001), and by April 2001 the company expected its first operating profit. Hotels became the engine: by 2003 Priceline was selling 1.5 million room nights a quarter, made a net profit, and carried out a one-for-six reverse split. From the December 2000 bottom the shares rose 343% in twelve months, and 85% in May 2003 alone.

The decisive move was small: in 2004-05 Priceline bought two European hotel booking sites, Active Hotels ($161 million) and Booking.com ($133 million). Booking.com became the core of the group, growing bookings by 40-60% a year through the late 2000s, even through the financial crisis, adding Agoda in Asia and Kayak (2013). The holding company was renamed The Priceline Group in 2014 and Booking Holdings in February 2018 (ticker BKNG). Covid-19 stopped travel in March 2020 (-21% in the month), but the recovery was quick, and by 2023 the group booked 1.235 billion room nights a year, earned $4.3 billion and began paying a dividend in 2024. A 25-for-1 share split followed in April 2026.

For a player the arc is a lesson in timing and survival: the IPO buyer of 1999 lost almost everything by 2002 and, in game prices, needed until 2011 to get back above the first month-end price, while the buyer at the bottom of December 2000 multiplied the money several hundredfold.

## Best and worst moments

- First weeks: 1999-04 (+96%), the high of the bubble.
- Worst stretch: -99% from 1999-04 to 2003-01; the collapse 2000-09 (-56%), 2000-10 (-52%), 2000-11 (-57%), 2000-12 (-48%).
- Best twelve months: +343% from 2000-12 to 2001-12, with 2001-01 (+102%), 2001-04 (+92%) and 2001-06 (+68%).
- Rebirth: 2003-05 (+85%) and 2003-07 (+47%) on hotel growth; 2005-07 Booking.com bought; 2018-02 renamed Booking Holdings.

## Unexplained moves

- 1999-04 (+96%): continuation of the post-IPO buying (the IPO was on March 30). The 10-Q for the March quarter mentions only a credit-card marketing programme with First USA from late April 1999; no other catalyst found.
- 2000-03 (+43%): no clear Priceline event; in March 2000 it licensed its model to MyPrice for Australia and New Zealand (10-Q for March 2000). The month coincides with the Nasdaq peak on March 10.
- 2001-01 (+102%): a rebound from the December 2000 low; the Delta restructuring (8-K of February 8, 2001) came in February. No January filing found; the market rose 3%.
- 2002-10 (+51%): a rebound with the market from the October 2002 low (market +9%); the third-quarter report came on November 6 (covered in 2002-11). No October event found.
