# Pets.com: from Super Bowl sock puppet to liquidation in nine months

Span: 2000-02 to 2000-11 (liquidation).

## The story

Pets.com was launched in November 1998, bought into in early 1999 by Julie Wainwright, and funded in March 1999 by Amazon.com (which took a majority stake at first) and venture investors. It sold pet food and supplies online with free or cheap shipping, and built one of the best-known brands of the boom around a sock-puppet mascot: a Macy's parade balloon in November 1999 and a $1.2 million Super Bowl advertisement in January 2000. When it listed on February 11, 2000 (7.5 million shares at $11, raising $82.5 million) it had 1999 sales of $5.8 million and a loss of $61.8 million, and Amazon still owned about 30%.

The business could not work at that scale. Pets.com was selling bulky, low-margin goods (cat litter, dog food) for less than they cost to buy and ship, and spending several times its sales on marketing: in the first quarter of 2000 it took in $7.7 million, spent $12.5 million on the goods and shipping and $28.9 million on marketing. It arrived on the market a month before the Nasdaq peaked; after Barron's warned on March 20 that internet companies were running out of cash, investors stopped funding such losses. The shares closed February at 7 and fell in every month but June.

The June deal for its rival Petstore.com and a promotion agreement with Discovery's television channels brought only a brief bounce. In August its quarterly report said the cash would last only into early 2001; in September it moved customer service to Indiana to save money and tried to sell itself, but more than 50 approached buyers and investors declined, and the board turned down an offer from PetSmart below the company's cash value. On November 7, 2000 Pets.com announced it would stop taking orders two days later and lay off 255 of its 320 staff. The shares, issued at $11, traded at about 19 cents. In December the company estimated that shareholders might eventually receive up to about 25 cents a share; the liquidation was completed in January 2001, and PetSmart bought the domain and brand.

Pets.com is in the game as the purest example of the bubble's failures: a famous brand, a big market and a business that lost money on every order. A player who bought at the IPO lost 96% within nine months.

## Best and worst moments

- Listing: 2000-02 at $11; the first month-end price was already 7.
- Worst months: 2000-11 (-44%), the shutdown; 2000-08 (-40%), the cash warning; 2000-03 (-39%), the Barron's "Burning Up" month.
- Only rising month: 2000-06 (+7%), the Petstore.com deal.
- Over its life: -96% from 2000-02 to 2000-11.
