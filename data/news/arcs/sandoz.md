# Sandoz: from Basel chemicals to a transplant-drug winner, a Rhine disaster, and the merger into Novartis

Span: 1979-12 to 1996-11 (merged with Ciba-Geigy into Novartis).

## The story

In 1979 Sandoz is one of Basel's three chemical and drug houses, a sprawling group of medicines, dyes and chemicals, farm chemicals and seeds, and food (Ovomaltine and Isostar through Wander, Wasa crispbread from 1982). Its future sits in a drug still in trials: cyclosporin, discovered in Sandoz's labs in 1972, used by Thomas Starzl in a liver transplant in March 1980 and approved by the FDA as Sandimmune in November 1983. It makes organ transplants work, and it makes Sandoz a growth stock.

From 1983 to 1987 the share nearly triples in dollars. The Swiss boom of 1985-86 and the falling dollar after the Plaza agreement add to it: +135% in the twelve months to April 1986, with three months of about +20% each (October and December 1985, April 1986). Then disaster: on 1 November 1986 a fire destroys the Schweizerhalle warehouse with about 1,350 tonnes of pesticides and farm chemicals, and the firefighting water carries the poison into the Rhine, turning it red and killing fish and eels down to the Netherlands. The share falls 17% in francs that month. Sandoz pays compensation, tightens its safety rules, and Switzerland writes a new ordinance on major accidents. The crash of October 1987 and the slide of Swiss bearer shares after Nestlé opened its register in November 1988 complete the worst stretch (-40% from September 1987 to March 1989).

The 1990s are about focus. Daniel Vasella, a doctor, runs Sandoz Pharma from 1992. Sandoz pays a record price for Gerber baby food in 1994 ($3.7 billion, 30 times profit), spins its chemicals off as Clariant in 1995, and then, on 7 March 1996, announces with its old rival Ciba-Geigy the largest corporate merger ever. The new company, Novartis, is chaired by Ciba's Alex Krauer and run by Vasella. Sandoz jumps 24% in March 1996 and trades for the last time at the end of November 1996, about eleven times its December 1979 price in dollars.

For the Swiss family Sandoz is a lesson in three things: how one drug can carry a company, how an accident can hit both a river and a share price, and how the franc layers over everything in a dollar-denominated game (March and April 1980, March 1995).

## Best and worst moments

- 1983-11: FDA approval of Sandimmune, the turning point.
- Best twelve months: +135% from 1985-04 to 1986-04; 1985-12 (+20%) on the year-end boom.
- 1986-11: -13% (-17% in francs), the Schweizerhalle fire.
- 1987-10: -17%, the crash; worst stretch -40% from 1987-09 to 1989-03.
- 1988-11: -11%, Swiss bearer shares slide after Nestlé's register opening.
- 1996-03: +24%, merger with Ciba-Geigy announced.
- 1996-09: all-time high in the game, just before the merger.
