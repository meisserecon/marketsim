# Schweizerischer Bankverein: the Basel bank that went global and took over its bigger rival from the inside

Span: 1982-03 to 1998-05.

## The story

The Schweizerischer Bankverein (Swiss Bank Corporation, SBC) was the middle one of the "big three" Swiss banks: second by assets behind the Bankgesellschaft, ahead of the Kreditanstalt, headquartered in Basel rather than Zurich. In the early 1980s the three lived in a comfortable world: a domestic banking cartel, bank secrecy confirmed by the voters in May 1984 (73% against the "Bankeninitiative"), and a steady flow of foreign money looking for safety. The Bankverein had the reputation of the most internationally minded of the three, and in the 1980s it expanded abroad (a portfolio-management company in New York in 1982, brokers in London and Paris in 1987). The 1988 drug-money laundering case, in which it held accounts for members of a Lebanese-Turkish ring, was a first crack in the respectable image of Swiss secrecy.

For a shareholder the Bankverein was above all a play on the Zurich market and the franc. The 1985 boom (best twelve months +120% from February 1985), the August 1986 rally on falling interest rates, the 1987 crash, the 1989 summer rally (with rumours of Japanese buyers), the May 1990 rebound: in all these months the Bankverein moved with the market, and in dollars the franc's swings added or subtracted several percentage points. The worst stretch, -51% from December 1986 to May 1989, was the crash plus a weak franc.

The 1990s were the decade of the Swiss property crash. The run on the Spar- und Leihkasse Thun in October 1991 opened a crisis in which many regional banks and finally the Volksbank lost their independence, and in which the big banks spent years provisioning for bad property loans. The Bankverein handled it better than the Bankgesellschaft, and when interest rates fell from late 1992 its share rallied hard: +58% between the end of 1992 and July 1993. Under the leadership that would later be associated with Marcel Ospel, the Bankverein meanwhile turned itself into an investment bank by acquisition: O'Connor & Associates (Chicago derivatives, 1992), Brinson Partners (asset management), S.G. Warburg (London, 1995, renamed SBC Warburg) and Dillon Read (New York, May 1997).

The end came by merger. On 8 December 1997 the Bankverein and the Bankgesellschaft announced a "merger of equals" under the name UBS: the Bankgesellschaft's shareholders got about 60% of the shares, but the Bankverein's managers got the key jobs, with Ospel as chief executive. The Bankverein's shareholders approved on 4 February 1998, the last general meeting was held in Basel in April, the last quote was on 29 May, and from the end of June each Bankverein share became 1 1/13 UBS shares. Over its sixteen years in the game the share rose about 4.5-fold in dollars (+356%). With hindsight the Bankverein's investment-banking strategy, carried into UBS, is the root of both UBS's global success and its near-collapse in 2008.

## Best and worst moments

- 1985-06 (+14%) and 1985-10 (+16%): the 1985 Zurich boom; best twelve months +120% from 1985-02 to 1986-02.
- 1986-08 (+15%): August rally on falling short-term rates.
- 1987-10 (-14%): the crash; start of the worst stretch, -51% from 1986-12 to 1989-05.
- 1989-06 (+17%) and 1989-07 (+16%): summer rally, Japanese-buyer rumours, strong franc.
- 1992-12 (+14%), 1993-07 (+15%): falling rates, the post-property-crisis rebound.
- 1995-03 (+14%): almost entirely the franc's jump against a collapsing dollar.
- 1997-12 (+8%): the merger with the Bankgesellschaft announced.

## Unexplained moves

- 1990-05 (+15%): explained only as a broad market rebound (NZZ weekly review of 30 June 1990 shows the SMI recovering from its April low; S&P 500 +9%). No Bankverein-specific news found. Beat kept with a move.
- 1992-09 (+15%): no cause found. Checked: NZZ of 1 October 1992 (no weekly review; business pages report a falling dollar and the European currency turmoil, but the franc gained only about 1% against the dollar that month); the NZZ year-end review of 31 December 1992 shows the SMI jumping in September but gives no reason. The ERM crisis of September 1992 is the likely background, but no source read links it to Swiss bank shares.
- 1997-04 (+15%): no cause found. Checked: NZZ of 2 May 1997 (daily report of 30 April: market at a high, Bankverein registered share at its year high of about 319-321 francs, no Bankverein news); Wikipedia and the encyclopedia histories (the Dillon Read purchase was agreed in May, not April).
