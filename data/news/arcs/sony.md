# Sony: Walkman, Hollywood, PlayStation, a lost decade and a comeback

Span: 1979-12 to today.

## The story

Sony enters the game months after launching the Walkman (July 1979), the archetype of Japanese
consumer electronics: Trinitron TVs, Betamax, and in 1982 the first CD player, developed with
Philips. The strong yen after the Plaza Accord of 1985 squeezed its export profits, and Sony's
answer under Norio Ohga was to buy content with its strong currency: CBS Records for 2 billion
dollars (November 1987) and Columbia Pictures (September 1989), the largest Japanese purchase of
an American company at the time. The film studio produced a 2.7 billion dollar write-off by 1994.

The PlayStation (announced October 1993, launched December 1994) rescued the 1990s. In the
internet bubble Sony was valued as a digital-media company; its ADR rose 55% in December 1999
alone and peaked in February 2000. Then came the lost decade: PlayStation 2 launch costs, the
flat-screen race lost to Korean and Japanese rivals, 20,000 job cuts in 2003, an expensive
PlayStation 3 (2006), the strong yen in the 2008 crisis, the 2011 PlayStation Network breach and a
520 billion yen loss in fiscal 2011. By November 2012 the ADR was 94% below its 2000 peak. The
first foreign chief, Howard Stringer (2005-2012), revived films and music but not electronics.

Kazuo Hirai (2012-2018) cut Vaio PCs and spun off TVs, kept entertainment despite activist Dan
Loeb's push to split it off (2013), and leaned on games, image sensors and music. Abenomics and a
weaker yen started the rerating in January 2013 (+33%). Under Kenichiro Yoshida Sony became Sony
Group (2021); by 2026 it was handing its television business to a TCL-led venture. For a dollar
player the yen matters both ways: a strong yen hurts Sony's profits but lifts its dollar price.

## Best and worst moments

- 1987-06: +30% and 1988-07: +33%, unexplained bubble-era jumps.
- 1987-11: +19% against a falling market, the CBS Records deal.
- 1999-12: +55%, the bubble peak month; peak in February 2000.
- 2001-07: -25% (unexplained) and 2001-09: -26% (September 11).
- 2003-04: -30%, unexplained in this arc (see below).
- 2000-02 to 2012-11: -94%, the lost decade; 2008-10 -25%, 2012-04 -22%.
- 2009-03 and 2009-04: +25% each, the post-crisis rebound and Stringer's reorganisation.
- 2013-01: +33%, Abenomics; 2013-05: +23%, Third Point's campaign.

## Unexplained moves

- 1975-02 (+40%): before the game starts in December 1979; not researched.
- 1987-06 (+30%): Tokyo was rising in the bubble (Nikkei past 25,000 on 3 June), but no
  Sony-specific news was found; the yen moved less than 2%.
- 1988-07 (+33%): checked Wikipedia and the UPI and CNN archive listings; no cause found.
- 2001-07 (-25%): the market was flat; Sony's first-quarter results (late July) could not be
  found in the CNN archive or Sony's press archive.
- 2003-04 (-30%): Sony normally reports annual results in late April, so the results are the
  likely trigger, but no source describing them or the reaction could be read (CNN Money, the Japan Times and Sony's IR
  archive were unavailable or incomplete in the Wayback Machine); the market rose 8%.
