# Tesla: from a money-losing sports-car start-up to a trillion-dollar bet on robots, with three brushes with death on the way

Span: 2010-06 to today.

## The story

Tesla comes into the game in June 2010 as a small Californian company that has sold a few thousand Roadsters, an electric sports car built on a Lotus chassis, and wants to build a family saloon. It loses money, lives on a 465 million dollar government loan, Daimler's and Toyota's money and the IPO proceeds of 226 million dollars. The share doubles in its first months on partnership news (Panasonic, Toyota's RAV4 EV), then drifts while the Model S is developed and launched (June 2012).

Early 2013 is the first near-death moment: the Model S ramp is slow and cash runs short, and Musk discusses a sale to Google. Instead, deliveries beat the target, Tesla reports its first profit and repays the government loan, and the share more than triples in two months (+42% in April, +81% in May 2013). Battery fires (November 2013) and the Gigafactory plan (February 2014) mark the next swings. The years 2014-2016 are spent on the Model X and the mass-market Model 3, unveiled with 325,000 reservations in 2016, and on the controversial purchase of Musk's SolarCity.

The Model 3 ramp of 2017-2019 is the second and third crisis: "production hell", a fatal Autopilot crash and a big recall (March 2018), the "funding secured" tweet and the SEC settlement that cost Musk the chairmanship (August-September 2018), and a fall to a three-year low in May 2019 amid the US-China trade war. Profits in late 2018 and from the second half of 2019, and the fast build-out of the Shanghai factory, turn the story.

Then comes the great rally: +1,004% from August 2019 to August 2020. Quarterly profits even in the pandemic, the title of most valuable car maker (July 2020), the five-for-one split (August, +74%), and the S&P 500 inclusion (November, +46%). The Hertz order of October 2021 takes Tesla past 1 trillion dollars. The bubble peaks in November 2021; Musk's purchase of Twitter, his share sales and rising competition halve the share, and December 2022 (-37%) is the worst month in the game.

Since 2023 the share follows Musk as much as the car business: price cuts and a rebound (January 2023), a growth warning (January 2024), the rally on Trump's election (November 2024), the brand damage of Musk's role in DOGE (February 2025, -28%), the robotaxi start in Austin and the feud with Trump (June 2025), Musk's billion-dollar share purchase (September 2025) and his trillion-dollar pay plan. In January 2026 BYD overtakes Tesla as the largest electric-car maker and Tesla drops the Model S and X to build Optimus robots. A weak second quarter and the SpaceX IPO push the share down 26% in July 2026; robotaxi permits and the AI-power theme lift it again in August. Over the whole game the share rises more than 200-fold.

## Best and worst moments

- Best months: 2013-05 +81% (first profit, loan repaid), 2020-08 +74% (split), 2010-11 +62%, 2020-01 +56%, 2020-04 +49%, 2020-11 +46% (S&P 500), 2021-10 +44% (Hertz, 1 trillion), 2013-04 +42%, 2023-01 +41%, 2024-11 +38% (Trump), 2014-02 +35%.
- Best twelve months: August 2019 to August 2020, +1,004%.
- Near-death moments: early 2013 (cash), 2018-03 -22% (production hell), 2019-05 -22% (three-year low).
- Worst stretch: -68% from 2021-11 to 2022-12; worst month 2022-12 -37% (Twitter).
- Turning points: 2017-07 (Model 3), 2020-07 (most valuable car maker), 2024-01 (growth warning; the story moves from cars to AI), 2026-01 (BYD ahead, Model S and X dropped).
- Latest: 2025-02 -28% (DOGE backlash), 2026-07 -26% (profit miss), 2026-08 +18%.

## Unexplained moves

None of the "Biggest months" is left unexplained. Not covered by beats: 2010-12 (-25%, giving back part of November's gain; no specific news found) and the 2026-05 rise (+14%; only modest news such as strong Chinese sales in April was found).
