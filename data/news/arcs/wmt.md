# Wal-Mart / Walmart: from small-town discounter to the trillion-dollar retailer

Span: 1979-12 (game start; listed since 1970) to today.

## The story

When the game opens, Wal-Mart is a fast-growing discount chain in the small towns of the American South, run from Bentonville, Arkansas, by its founder Sam Walton. It has 276 stores and is about to pass a billion dollars in yearly sales, a mark no retailer reached faster. Its formula, low prices on high volume with tight costs and its own distribution network, produced unusually high returns, and the 1980s were its golden decade on the stock market: the price rose more than fortyfold from the end of 1979 to the end of 1989, with the best twelve months of all (+201%) in the 1982-83 bull market. The rise was interrupted only by the October 1987 crash, which took 41% off the price in three months; the business never faltered and the stock recovered within two years.

The milestones of the climb are the purchase of Kuhn's Big K (1981), the first Sam's Club (1983), Forbes naming Sam Walton the richest American (1985), the first Supercenter (1988) and, in March 1991, overtaking Sears and Kmart as the largest retailer in the United States. Walton handed day-to-day control to David Glass in 1988 and died in April 1992.

After Walton's death growth slowed, the 'Buy American' campaign was questioned on television, and the stock went sideways to down from 1992 to 1996. Growth now had to come from new formats (Supercenters, Sam's Club, the Pace clubs) and new countries: Mexico (1991), Canada (1994), China (1996), Germany (1997) and Britain's Asda (1999). In 1997 Wal-Mart replaced Woolworth in the Dow, and from the end of 1996 to the end of 1999 the stock rose about sixfold as investors paid growth-stock prices for its steady earnings; it was the best Dow stock of 1998.

The peak around the turn of the millennium, when Lee Scott took over from Glass, was followed by a lost decade for shareholders. Wal-Mart topped the Fortune 500, but faced the largest sex-discrimination class action in US history (2004-2011), criticism of its wages, a costly exit from Germany (2006) and the Mexican bribery scandal (2012). Its defensive quality showed in 2008, when it rose while the market crashed. Doug McMillon, chief executive from 2014, raised wages and invested heavily in online shopping; the October 2015 profit warning cost 10% in a day and the stock lost 29% that year.

The investments paid off in the 2020s. Jet.com (2016), Flipkart (2018), the pandemic surge in online orders (2020), advertising and memberships turned Walmart into a retail platform that investors valued more like a technology company, despite the inflation shock of May 2022 (worst day since 1987). The stock rose about 70% in 2024, Walmart moved its listing to Nasdaq in December 2025, John Furner succeeded McMillon in February 2026, and that month Walmart became the first traditional retailer worth more than a trillion dollars.

## Best and worst moments

- 1982-07 to 1983-07: best twelve months, +201%; 1982-11 +21% in the retail-led bull market.
- 1987-08 to 1987-11: worst stretch, -41%; 1987-10 -27% in the crash.
- 1991-03: overtakes Sears as the largest US retailer (turning point).
- 1992-04: Sam Walton dies; the stock stalls from late 1992 to 1996.
- 1997-1999: the stock rises about sixfold; 1998-10 +26%, 1999-12 +20%.
- 2000-01: -21%, the start of a decade without gains.
- 2015-10: profit warning on higher wages, -10% in a day; 2015 -29%.
- 2018-02: -16% on slower online growth.
- 2022-05: -16%, worst day since October 1987.
- 2024: about +70%; 2026-02 valued above one trillion dollars.
