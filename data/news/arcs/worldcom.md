# LDDS / WorldCom: a decade of takeovers paid in stock, then the largest accounting fraud and bankruptcy of its time

Span: 1990-01 to 2002-07 (bankruptcy). Names: LDDS Communications to 1995-04, WorldCom from 1995-05, MCI
WorldCom from 1998-09, WorldCom again from 2000-05.

## The story

LDDS began in 1983 in a Hattiesburg coffee shop as a reseller of cheap long-distance calls to small
businesses in Mississippi, and from 1985 was run by Bernard Ebbers, a former basketball coach and motel
owner. Its method never changed: buy a rival, pay with LDDS shares, use the bigger company's higher share
price to buy the next one. In the 1990s it bought more than sixty companies, from Advanced
Telecommunications (1992) and the Metromedia and Resurgens phone businesses (1993) to the international
carrier IDB (1994), the WilTel network (1995) and MFS with the internet backbone UUNet (1996). By 1996 the
Wall Street Journal reported returns to shareholders of 57% a year for a decade; in the game the stock rose
about ninetyfold from 1990 to its peak in 1999.

The crowning deal was MCI, a company three times WorldCom's size, bought for $37 billion in stock in 1998.
Salomon Smith Barney's telecom analyst Jack Grubman advised on the deals, sat in on board meetings and kept
telling investors to buy. In October 1999 MCI WorldCom agreed to buy Sprint for $115 billion; in July 2000
regulators in Washington and Brussels killed the deal, and with it the strategy. Long-distance prices were
falling, many of WorldCom's customers among the new telecom companies were going bankrupt, and there was no
next acquisition to keep growth going.

From the second half of 2000 the warning signs were public. In November 2000 WorldCom wrote off $685
million of unpaid bills, announced it would split into two tracking stocks, and disclosed that its board had
lent Ebbers money so he would not have to sell shares to meet margin calls; the stock fell 37%. In February
2002 it disclosed it had already paid $199 million to cover his loans. In April 2002 the SEC was
investigating, Grubman finally downgraded, and the board pushed Ebbers out; in May WorldCom was cut to junk
and scrapped a dividend. Behind the numbers, the company had since 2001 been booking billions of ordinary
line costs as investments to keep reporting profits. The new auditor's arrival and an internal audit team
found it: on 25 June 2002 WorldCom admitted $3.8 billion of false profits (later about $11 billion), and on
21 July it filed for the largest bankruptcy in U.S. history. Ebbers was later sentenced to 25 years.

For players the lesson is the same as Enron's but louder: when a company's growth depends on acquisitions
paid in its own stock, the stock price is holding the whole structure up, and a board that lends the chief
executive money to avoid selling his shares is telling you how fragile that is.

## Best and worst moments

- 1990-06, 1991-01, 1991-07: +30%, +31%, +26%, the early run (see Unexplained moves).
- 1997-05: +23% (unexplained).
- 1999-06: the peak, $64.50 on 21 June 1999 (actual quote; the game's split-adjusted price differs).
- 2000-11: -37%, tracking stocks, bad-debt charge, loans to Ebbers.
- 2001-01: +53%, rebound on the Fed's surprise rate cut.
- 2002-01: -29%, Global Crossing bankruptcy and post-Enron fears.
- 2002-02: -25%, the $199 million paid for Ebbers' margin loans.
- 2002-04: -63%, Ebbers out, SEC investigating, Grubman downgrades.
- 2002-05: -33%, junk rating, dividend scrapped.
- 2002-06: -50%, the $3.8 billion fraud.
- 2002-07: -83%, bankruptcy.
- Best twelve months: +195% from 1990-02 to 1991-02. Worst stretch: -100% from 1999-03 to 2002-07.

## Unexplained moves

- 1990-06 (+30%), 1991-01 (+31%), 1991-07 (+26%): no company-specific news found for these months; Wikipedia
  and the PBS Frontline chronology give only years for LDDS's early acquisitions. Note also that the price
  data for 1990-1992 is unverified (data/manual/FEASIBILITY.md: it is not known whether the series follows
  LDDS or Resurgens before 1993, and one split step is undocumented), so these moves may be partly artefacts.
  Beat 1991-07 reports the run-up itself without a cause.
- 1997-05 (+23%, market +6%): no cause found. Checked the PBS Frontline chronology, Wikipedia (MCI Inc.,
  Bernard Ebbers) and the LA Times archive references; WorldCom's 1997 SEC filings were not searched in full.
