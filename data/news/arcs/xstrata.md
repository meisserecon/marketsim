# Xstrata: Glencore's coal spin-off becomes a mining giant on China's boom, nearly breaks in 2008, and is swallowed by its parent

Span: 2002-05 to 2013-04 (merged into Glencore, 3.05 Glencore shares per Xstrata share).

## The story

Xstrata began as Südelektra, a Swiss infrastructure holding that Marc Rich's trading house controlled from 1990 and turned into a small miner of ferroalloys and zinc. In 2001 it hired Mick Davis, the Billiton finance director who had engineered the BHP Billiton merger, as chief executive. When Glencore's own plan to float its coal mines (Enex) collapsed after 11 September 2001, Davis offered to buy them. In March 2002 Xstrata relaunched in London and bought Glencore's Australian and South African coal, becoming the world's largest exporter of thermal coal. Glencore kept about 40%, later a third, and the right to market much of the output. That relationship defined Xstrata to the end: a listed miner with a trader as its largest shareholder and customer.

Davis then made three transforming acquisitions at the right moments. MIM Holdings (2003, about US$3bn) brought Mount Isa's copper and Queensland coking coal just before the metals cycle turned; copper doubled within two years. After losing WMC and its Olympic Dam mine to BHP in 2005 rather than overpay, he bought Falconbridge in 2006 for more than C$20bn in cash at C$62.50 a share, beating Inco, and became a top-five producer of copper, nickel and zinc as well as coal and ferrochrome. China's industrialisation did the rest: coal went from $25 a tonne to $180, copper from $1,600 to about $8,700, and Xstrata's share price rose more than fifteenfold from its March 2003 low to mid-2008. Brazil's Vale offered a deal worth up to $90bn in early 2008; it failed over Glencore's insistence on keeping its marketing rights.

The crash was brutal. Xstrata had just bid £5bn for Lonmin and was building a 24.9% stake in it when Lehman failed. Copper fell 63% between July and December 2008, the shares lost 90% between June 2008 and January 2009, and in January 2009 Xstrata launched a £4.1bn rights issue at a 66% discount, cut its dividend and capital spending, and at the same time bought Glencore's Colombian coal mines (Prodeco) for $2bn, money Glencore used to take up its own rights. Shareholders were diluted, but the balance sheet was fixed just before China's stimulus set off a violent recovery: the shares rose about 240% in 2009.

From 2010 Xstrata traded as a bet on copper and coal and on the euro crisis, with big swings in 2010, 2011 and 2012. Glencore floated in May 2011 and in February 2012 proposed a "merger of equals" at 2.8 of its shares per Xstrata share, with Davis to run the combined group. Qatar Holding, with over 10% of Xstrata, held out for more; Glencore raised to 3.05 in September 2012 but made Ivan Glasenberg chief executive and pushed Davis out. China approved the deal in April 2013 on condition that Las Bambas in Peru be sold, and Xstrata disappeared into Glencore Xstrata on 2 May 2013, near the end of the commodity boom. Over its eleven years in the game the share price (in dollars) rose about 236%, with almost all of the gain made by 2008 and given back twice.

## Best and worst moments

- Listing, 2002-05: a cheap coal miner with a dealmaker CEO, just before the boom.
- First crash, 2002-07: -29% in a month with no company news; markets and coal both falling.
- MIM deal, 2003-04 and 2003-05: +18% and +24%; the copper rally of 2003-10 added +33%.
- Peak, 2008-06: about $45 a share in the game's dollars, up about fifteenfold from early 2003.
- Worst stretch, 2008-06 to 2009-01: -90%, including 2008-09 -45%, 2008-10 -44%, 2008-12 -34%.
- Turning point, 2009-01: the £4.1bn rescue rights issue; best twelve months followed, +255% from 2009-01 to 2010-01 (2009-04 +35%, 2009-05 +23%, 2009-07 +25%).
- Euro crisis swing, 2011-09 -27% then 2011-10 +32%.
- Merger announced, 2012-02 (+13%); Grexit month 2012-05 -26%.
- Exit, 2013-04 (-8%): Chinese approval and Davis's departure; holders become Glencore shareholders at 3.05 to one.

## Unexplained moves

No biggest month is left entirely without a beat, but three rest on market and commodity context only, with no company-specific news found:

- 2002-07 (-29%): checked Wikipedia, the 2009 circular's history and encyclopedia.com; nothing company-specific. Explained by the July 2002 market crash and the fall in coal prices.
- 2003-10 (+33%): no announcement found; explained by the copper, nickel and zinc rally (World Bank data).
- 2009-05 (+23%) and 2009-07 (+25%): commodity rebound only. A reported June 2009 merger approach to Anglo American could not be confirmed from a readable source and is not used.

The Xstrata RNS archive was not reachable (Investegate only holds Glencore's), and the web search quota ran out during research, so 2002-2010 company news is thinner than for Glencore.
