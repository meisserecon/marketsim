# Thread `asia-ltcm`: Asia, Russia, LTCM

East Asia's 'tigers' and 'tiger cubs' grew by up to ten percent a year into the mid-1990s, much of it financed with short-term foreign loans in dollars that seemed safe because their currencies were pegged to the dollar; a good part went into property. In May 1997 speculators attacked the Thai baht, and on 2 July Thailand had to let it float. The panic spread from Thailand to Malaysia, Indonesia and the Philippines, hit multinationals like Coca-Cola, crashed Hong Kong's market in October (and Wall Street for a day) and forced South Korea, a rich industrial economy, to the IMF in December. Falling commodity prices and fleeing capital then broke Russia, which defaulted in August 1998. The flight to safety blew apart the bets of Long-Term Capital Management, a hugely leveraged hedge fund; the New York Fed organised a rescue by 14 banks in September 1998, and UBS, through an option deal struck in 1997, became the largest single loser, with a loss of about 950 million francs that cost its chairman his job. Fed rate cuts calmed markets in the autumn of 1998, and by 1999 Asia was recovering.
