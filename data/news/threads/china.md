# Thread `china`: the rise of China

China opened in December 1978 under Deng Xiaoping, with special economic zones such as Shenzhen next to Hong Kong. The crushing of the Tiananmen protests in June 1989 stalled the reforms until Deng's southern tour of 1992 restarted them; growth reached about 13% a year. In July 1997 Hong Kong, a capitalist financial centre, returned to China under 'one country, two systems'. Joining the WTO in December 2001 made China the factory of the world: it made most of the world's DVD players by 2002, on thin margins and with the patents held abroad, and its companies began to buy Western brands (Lenovo took IBM's PCs in 2004). Its appetite for metals and oil drove the commodity boom; the Beijing Olympics of 2008 were its showcase, and its 4 trillion yuan stimulus of November 2008 pulled it through the financial crisis. In 2010 it passed Japan as the second-largest economy at market exchange rates (reported February 2011), and in October 2014 IMF figures made it the largest by purchasing power, while the US stayed ahead at market exchange rates. The 2015 crash and devaluation showed the world how much it now depended on China; from 2018 the US answered with a trade war and, from 2022, a ban on advanced chips. The Evergrande default of 2021 opened a long property bust, yet Chinese technology kept catching up: DeepSeek in AI in January 2025, tariffs of 145% in April 2025, and BYD overtaking Tesla as the biggest electric-car maker in 2025.
