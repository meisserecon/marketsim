# Thread: housing-crisis

Span: 2003-06 to 2009-12.

After the dot-com bust the Fed cut its rate to 1 percent in June 2003, and mortgages became the cheapest on record. Home prices took off: by mid-2005 the median existing home cost 14.7 percent more than a year earlier, lenders were selling interest-only and other exotic adjustable loans, and about a fifth of new mortgages went to subprime borrowers with weak credit. Greenspan saw "froth"; the Fed raised rates seventeen times from 2004 to 2006, but long-term mortgage rates barely moved until the boom ran out of buyers. In autumn 2006 national house prices fell year on year for the first time in decades. In March 2007 New Century, the biggest independent subprime lender, stopped lending; in July two Bear Stearns hedge funds full of subprime securities were wiped out; in August BNP Paribas froze its funds and the interbank market seized up. From then on the losses showed up in the banks: Citigroup's write-downs (November 2007), Bear Stearns's forced sale (March 2008), the takeover of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and the bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers (September 2008), and the state rescues of UBS (October) and Citigroup (November). The aftermath is zero interest rates, mass bond buying and unemployment near 10 percent in 2009, while shares recover from March. The rates thread carries the Fed's side from December 2008.
