# Thread `japan`: Japan's rise, the bubble and the lost decade

In 1980 Japan was the world's admired export machine: it overtook the United States as the biggest car maker and its cameras, televisions and chips won markets everywhere, which brought trade quarrels with Washington. The Plaza Accord of September 1985 pushed the dollar down from about 240 yen; to cushion exporters from the soaring yen, the Bank of Japan cut rates to record lows, and the cheap money flowed into shares and land. The Nikkei passed 20,000 in January 1987 and 30,000 in December 1988, Japanese banks became the biggest in the world, and Japanese buyers took Rockefeller Center. The Bank of Japan began raising rates in May 1989; the Nikkei peaked at almost 39,000 on 29 December 1989. In 1990 the crash began, by August 1992 shares had more than halved, and the bad loans of the bubble surfaced slowly: failing credit cooperatives and the jusen in 1995, the collapse of Yamaichi and Hokkaido Takushoku in November 1997, the nationalisation of the Long-Term Credit Bank in 1998, public capital and zero interest rates in 1999, defensive mega-mergers such as Mizuho. The Nikkei touched bottom below 8,000 in March 2003, a fifth of its peak. Within the thread every beat tells only what was known in its month; the wider arc continues later (Abenomics, the 2024 record) outside the thread.
